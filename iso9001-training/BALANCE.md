## Reading the results

- Controls always beat no controls with the default parameters. The gap is large because every escape costs 15 points and one reputation point (worth 3 points at the end), and an uncontrolled team has many escapes.
- Purchase order matters. The six core controls capture most of the benefit, and random or cheapest-first ordering scores noticeably lower than priority ordering. Table 2a shows that every control helps on its own; table 2b shows which ones are good uses of scarce QP. Use both in the debrief: for example, discuss why C10, C11 and C07 repay little in this model (they pay back through audit points, recurrence and early detection rather than direct savings), and why C09 is worth far more than its price.
- Tuning suggestion: C07 (in-process check at the constraint) is the weakest purchase under the default parameters. To make the constraint lesson stronger, raise `inProcessDetect`, raise the cost of defects found after the constraint (`constraintScrapExtra`), or set `workItemsReleasedPerRound` above the constraint capacity.
- The parameters that move outcomes most are the escape penalty, the stage defect probabilities, and the rate of erroneous release. To make the game closer, reduce the escape penalty or the defect probabilities in the configuration box on the setup screen.
- Team choices change dice outcomes only through the controls owned, so the same seed with different choices shows the value of each control.
