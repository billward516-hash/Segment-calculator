# Micro-session Series (Format D)

*This material is an original training aid. It is not a substitute for the standard. Obtain ISO 9001 from ISO or a national standards body.*

This series gives {{COMPANY}} supervisors and team leaders 24 short sessions, one per week, to run at shift start or in a team meeting. Each session takes 15 to 20 minutes and covers one topic, one example, one question, and one action. Clause numbers follow ISO 9001:2015.

## How to run a session

**Standard plan (15 to 20 minutes).**

| Time | Step | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:02 | Topic | State the topic and why it matters here. |
| 0:02 to 0:07 | Example | Present the example. Replace it with a real one from {{COMPANY}} where approved. |
| 0:07 to 0:12 | Question | Ask the question. Take two or three answers. Confirm the answer. |
| 0:12 to 0:15 | Action | State the action for this shift. Ask each person to confirm it. |
| 0:15 to 0:20 | Optional | Run the activity listed for the session. |

**Preparation.** Read the session and the module it draws on. Bring the item named under "Bring". Replace the fictional example with a real one where the area supervisor and quality approve, and remove names.

**Records.** Record date, session number, facilitator, and attendees on the sign-in sheet. Micro-sessions build awareness. They do not establish competence for a task on their own.

## Session 1: Why the QMS exists

- **Module:** 0 | **Clause:** 4 to 10 overview | **Bring:** a completed check sheet or ticket from the area
- **Example.** A customer audit at Meridian Group sampled one operator's check sheet. It was the only proof that the batch had been measured before release.
- **Question.** Who creates most of the evidence in our quality system?
- **Answer.** Frontline staff, through the records they make while doing the work.
- **Action.** Complete every record on this shift at the time of the activity.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Example with the check sheet passed around (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: each person names one record they made yesterday (5).

## Session 2: The quality policy in plain language

- **Module:** 2 | **Clause:** 5.2 | **Bring:** the current quality policy **[OWNER INPUT]**
- **Example.** A Meridian Group warehouse worker explains the policy as "scan every pallet before it is loaded, every time".
- **Question.** What does our policy ask of you on a normal shift?
- **Answer.** Any accurate action linked to the policy commitments: meeting requirements and improving. The wording need not match.
- **Action.** Tell a colleague, in one sentence, what the policy means at your station.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Read the policy aloud (5) | Question, three answers (5) | Action (3) | Optional: pairs write their sentence on a card for the board (5).

## Session 3: Our objectives and my contribution

- **Module:** 3 | **Clause:** 6.2 | **Bring:** the objectives chart for the area **[OWNER INPUT]**
- **Example.** A Meridian Group team's on-time delivery objective dropped for two weeks because of waiting time at one step.
- **Question.** Which objective does your work influence, and how?
- **Answer.** Any current objective with a concrete link, such as first-time-right rate through correct setup.
- **Action.** Look at the objectives chart once on this shift and note the latest result.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Walk through the chart (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: identify one thing that would move the result (5).

## Session 4: Who has authority to stop the line

- **Module:** 2 | **Clause:** 5.3 | **Bring:** the stop-work rule **[OWNER INPUT]**
- **Example.** A Meridian Group technician sees a reading outside the limit, stops the process, and calls the supervisor, who thanks them.
- **Question.** If you see nonconforming output being produced, what do you do and whom do you call?
- **Answer.** Stop the process if you have the authority, or call the supervisor at once, and keep the work item on hold. Follow the local rule.
- **Action.** Confirm the name and contact of the person you call when you need to stop work.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Example (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: walk to where the contact list is posted (5).

## Session 5: Reading a work instruction: current revision check

- **Module:** 4 | **Clause:** 7.5 | **Bring:** a current instruction and an obsolete copy marked "training only"
- **Example.** At Meridian Group a photocopy of revision C was used after revision D changed a setting. Twelve work items were reworked.
- **Question.** How do you confirm that the instruction in front of you is current?
- **Answer.** Check its revision identifier against the controlled list or system, and confirm it is the issued copy at the point of use.
- **Action.** Check the revision of one instruction you use today.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Compare the two copies (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: search the area for uncontrolled copies and remove them (5).

## Session 6: Recording data correctly

- **Module:** 4 | **Clause:** 7.5 | **Bring:** a blank log sheet or a screen view of a record
- **Example.** A Meridian Group log was found pre-filled for the rest of the week. The auditor raised a finding and the batch records were questioned.
- **Question.** What makes a record trustworthy?
- **Answer.** It is made at the time of the activity, legible, accurate, attributed with initials or user identification, and dated. Corrections are visible and initialed.
- **Action.** Make every entry on this shift at the time of the activity.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Example (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: show the correct way to correct an entry (5).

## Session 7: Calibration labels and what they mean

- **Module:** 4 | **Clause:** 7.1.5 | **Bring:** a measuring or test resource with its status label or record
- **Example.** A Meridian Group scale was found out of tolerance. All weighings since its last valid check were reviewed.
- **Question.** What do you do if a measuring resource is overdue for calibration or verification?
- **Answer.** Do not use it. Flag it and report it so that earlier results can be assessed.
- **Action.** Check the status of every measuring resource you use before first use on this shift.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Read the label together (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: each person checks one resource in the area (5).

## Session 8: Training records and qualification

- **Module:** 4 | **Clause:** 7.2 | **Bring:** the training matrix for the area
- **Example.** A Meridian Group new starter covered a task for an absent colleague without sign-off. The output was correct, but there was no evidence of competence.
- **Question.** How do you know you are qualified for a task?
- **Answer.** The training matrix or record shows sign-off for that task. If not, ask before performing it.
- **Action.** Find your name on the training matrix and check that it is correct.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Example (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: report any errors on the matrix to the supervisor (5).

## Session 9: Handling customer property (material, data, records, premises)

- **Module:** 6 | **Clause:** 8.5.3 | **Bring:** an example of how customer property is identified here
- **Example.** A Meridian Group client file was sent to the wrong recipient. The company reported the loss to the client and recorded it.
- **Question.** Name two kinds of customer property in our area.
- **Answer.** Any two of: customer material or equipment, customer data and records, specifications, samples, intellectual property, personal information, customer premises.
- **Action.** Keep each customer's property identified and separate on this shift.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Example (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: locate where customer property is stored or held in the area (5).

## Session 10: Work item identification and status marking

- **Module:** 6 | **Clause:** 8.5.2 | **Bring:** status labels or a screen showing status
- **Example.** Two Meridian Group work items without status marking were mixed. One was untested and was delivered.
- **Question.** What must be recorded when a work item changes status?
- **Answer.** The new status, the date and time, who made the change, and the work item identification.
- **Action.** Confirm that every work item at your station shows its identification and current status.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Show the labels or screen (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: quick check of five work items in the area (5).

## Session 11: Traceability: tracing a work item backward and forward

- **Module:** 6 | **Clause:** 8.5.2 | **Bring:** the records for one completed work item
- **Example.** A defective input batch at Meridian Group was traced to six work items in two hours because every record carried the batch identifier.
- **Question.** What does traceability allow the company to do?
- **Answer.** Trace back to inputs, equipment, and people, and forward to customers, so that a problem is contained to the work items affected.
- **Action.** Record input batch or version identifiers on every work item record on this shift.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Trace the work item together (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: identify one record gap that would widen a recall (5).

## Session 12: STOP, FLAG, HOLD, REPORT

- **Module:** 7 | **Clause:** 8.7 | **Bring:** the STOP, FLAG, HOLD, REPORT card and a blank hold tag
- **Example.** A Meridian Group developer blocked a release after a failed test, tagged the build, and reported it. The customer never saw the fault.
- **Question.** What are the four steps, in order?
- **Answer.** STOP, FLAG, HOLD, REPORT. No rework, redo, or scrap without a documented disposition.
- **Action.** Locate the hold area or hold status for your station.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Example (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: one person completes a hold tag for a mock work item (5).

## Session 13: Correction versus corrective action

- **Module:** 9 | **Clause:** 10.2 | **Bring:** a closed nonconformance record (names removed)
- **Example.** At Meridian Group, a mislabeled carton was relabeled (correction). The label template was then locked to prevent manual edits (corrective action).
- **Question.** What is the difference between a correction and a corrective action?
- **Answer.** A correction fixes the nonconforming output. A corrective action removes the cause so that it does not recur.
- **Action.** When you report a problem, add one idea about its cause.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Example (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: sort five actions into correction or corrective action (5).

## Session 14: 5 Whys practice

- **Module:** 9 | **Clause:** 10.2 | **Bring:** a flip chart or whiteboard
- **Example.** A Meridian Group work item failed a final check. Five whys led from "cleaning skipped" to "the change process had no review step".
- **Question.** Why is "operator error" not enough as a root cause?
- **Answer.** It names the symptom. The useful question is why the system allowed the error.
- **Action.** Ask "why did the system allow this?" the next time a problem is discussed.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Build a 5 Whys on a recent minor problem (8) | Question (3) | Action (2) | Optional: check that the last why is a system condition (5).

## Session 15: Change control: what is a change?

- **Module:** 3 | **Clause:** 6.3, 8.5.6 | **Bring:** the change request form or system screen **[OWNER INPUT]**
- **Example.** A Meridian Group team switched to a new cleaning agent without review. Residue caused test failures two weeks later.
- **Question.** What must happen before a process setting is changed?
- **Answer.** The change is reviewed and authorized, with consequences, resources, and responsibilities considered, and the change is recorded.
- **Action.** Raise a change request for any change you plan, however small.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Example (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: list three changes that would need review in this area (5).

## Session 16: External providers: receiving inspection

- **Module:** 5 | **Clause:** 8.4 | **Bring:** a delivery note and the receiving checklist
- **Example.** A Meridian Group delivery had the right part number but the wrong revision. Receiving verification caught it before use.
- **Question.** What is verified when an externally provided input is received?
- **Answer.** That it meets the specified requirements: identity, quantity, condition, version, and required documents.
- **Action.** Check identity and version of every input you use on this shift before use.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Walk through the checklist (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: name one external provider that is not a material supplier (5).

## Session 17: Work environment: conditions that affect quality and safety

- **Module:** 4 | **Clause:** 7.1.4 | **Bring:** the environment record for the area, if one exists
- **Example.** A Meridian Group area ran above its temperature limit for a shift. Outputs made in that period were held and reviewed.
- **Question.** Name two conditions in our area that affect quality.
- **Answer.** Any two relevant conditions: temperature, humidity, cleanliness, lighting, noise, workload, respectful communication.
- **Action.** Report any condition outside its limit as soon as you see it.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Example (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: check the current readings together (5).

## Session 18: Preservation, handling, and packaging

- **Module:** 6 | **Clause:** 8.5.4 | **Bring:** the handling or packaging instruction
- **Example.** Meridian Group outputs were stacked above the permitted height and the lower cartons were crushed in transit.
- **Question.** What protects an output between our last check and the customer?
- **Answer.** Correct identification, handling, packaging, storage, and protection as the instruction states.
- **Action.** Follow the handling and packaging instruction for every output on this shift.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Example (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: inspect one packed or staged output against the instruction (5).

## Session 19: Release: who may authorize

- **Module:** 6 | **Clause:** 8.6 | **Bring:** the release authority list **[OWNER INPUT]**
- **Example.** Under deadline pressure, a Meridian Group item with an open discrepancy was released without authority. The customer returned it.
- **Question.** Who may authorize release of a work item with an open discrepancy?
- **Answer.** Only a person with defined release authority and, where applicable, the customer, with a record.
- **Action.** If you are asked to release an item with an open check, refer it to the authorized person.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Example (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: find the release authority list (5).

## Session 20: Customer complaints: what happens next

- **Module:** 8, 9 | **Clause:** 9.1.2, 10.2 | **Bring:** a closed complaint record (customer name removed)
- **Example.** A Meridian Group complaint about a missing document led to containment, a root cause, and a change to the release checklist.
- **Question.** What should you do if a customer tells you about a problem?
- **Answer.** Record it and pass it to the complaint process at once. Do not promise an outcome.
- **Action.** Know where and how to log a complaint you receive.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Follow the complaint record step by step (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: discuss what the customer saw at each step (5).

## Session 21: Internal audits: a helpful process

- **Module:** 8 | **Clause:** 9.2 | **Bring:** the internal audit schedule
- **Example.** A Meridian Group internal audit found an overdue verification before the certification audit did. It was corrected in a week.
- **Question.** Why must internal auditors be impartial?
- **Answer.** An auditor cannot fairly judge their own work. Impartiality keeps the results credible.
- **Action.** Note when the next internal audit of your area is due.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Example (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: name one record an auditor would ask for in this area (5).

## Session 22: Responding to an auditor

- **Module:** 10 | **Clause:** 9.2 | **Bring:** the audit response card
- **Example.** A Meridian Group operator was asked where the current instruction was kept. The operator opened the controlled system and showed the revision.
- **Question.** What should you say if you do not know the answer?
- **Answer.** "I will find out," and tell your supervisor. Do not guess.
- **Action.** Keep the audit response card where you can see it.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Read the card aloud (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: pairs practice one auditor question and answer (5).

## Session 23: Risk-based thinking at my station

- **Module:** 3 | **Clause:** 6.1 | **Bring:** the risk list for the area, if one exists
- **Example.** A Meridian Group bottleneck machine had no spare for a critical part. A spare was stocked after a risk review.
- **Question.** Give one risk at your station and one control for it.
- **Answer.** Any realistic pair, such as tool drift controlled by calibration and a status check before use.
- **Action.** Tell your supervisor one risk that is not yet controlled.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Example (5) | Question (5) | Action (3) | Optional: add the risks raised to the area list (5).

## Session 24: Review and recognition of improvements

- **Module:** 9 | **Clause:** 10.1, 10.3 | **Bring:** a list of improvements made in the area over the series
- **Example.** Meridian Group teams presented three improvements that had held for three months, with before and after results.
- **Question.** How do we know an improvement worked?
- **Answer.** Results reviewed after a set period show that the problem has not recurred or the measure has improved, and the outcome is recorded.
- **Action.** Propose one improvement for the next quarter.
- **Plan.** Topic (2) | Review the list and thank contributors (6) | Question (4) | Action (3) | Optional: Clause Sprint 5-minute round as a series close (5).
