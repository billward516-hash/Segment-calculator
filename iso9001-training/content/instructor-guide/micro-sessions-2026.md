## Session 25: Quality culture in practice: what it looks like at my station

**Objective.** Each participant can describe two behaviors at their own station that show a strong quality culture.

**Materials.** Culture Under Pressure card CU02 (printed or on screen); flip chart.

| Time | Step | Script and notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:03 | Idea | "The new edition is reported to expect leaders to promote and show a quality culture and ethical behavior (5.1.1, High, unverified), and everyone to be aware of it (7.3, High). Quality culture means the habits that decide what we do about quality when nobody is checking." |
| 0:03 to 0:08 | Example | Read CU02 (the record that does not match). Ask: "What is the right entry? What made the wrong entry tempting?" |
| 0:08 to 0:15 | Question | "What does a strong quality culture look like at your station? Name one behavior you see and one you would like to see more." Write answers in two columns. |
| 0:15 to 0:20 | Action | Each person picks one behavior to practice this week. Supervisor notes one obstacle to remove. |

**Quick check.** True or false: the culture expectation asks operators to adopt personal beliefs. *Answer: false. It asks leaders to create conditions where the right action is the easy one, and everyone to act honestly in records and reporting.*

## Session 26: Speaking up: reporting without blame

**Objective.** Each participant can name the reporting channel and explain why near-misses are reported.

**Materials.** Culture Under Pressure card CU04; STOP, FLAG, HOLD, REPORT card. **[OWNER INPUT]** the company non-punitive reporting statement and reporting channel.

| Time | Step | Script and notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:03 | Idea | "A report is information, not a complaint. The reported 2026 culture expectation (5.1.1, High) is shown most clearly by what happens after someone speaks up." |
| 0:03 to 0:08 | Example | Read CU04 (the near-miss nobody reported). Ask the operator and supervisor questions only. |
| 0:08 to 0:15 | Question | "What happens here when someone reports a problem that delays a shipment? What would make reporting easier?" |
| 0:15 to 0:20 | Action | Confirm the channel. Read the non-punitive reporting statement. Recognize one recent report (fictional if none is approved for sharing). |

**Facilitator rule.** If discussion turns to a real person or incident, stop it and return to the card. Refer individual concerns to the supervisor or Human Resources privately.

**Quick check.** Why report a near-miss that caused no defect? *Answer: so the cause can be fixed before it produces a real defect or escape.*

## Session 27: Risks and opportunities at my station

**Objective.** Each participant can name one risk and one opportunity at their station.

**Materials.** Worked example table (Appendix A.5.2); scenarios S104 and S110; sticky notes in two colors.

| Time | Step | Script and notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:03 | Idea | "Risks could stop us reaching our results. Opportunities could improve them. The 2026 edition is reported to address them more distinctly, in 6.1.1 to 6.1.3 (High; numbering unverified). In 2015, 6.1 already covers both." |
| 0:03 to 0:08 | Example | Compare S104 (risks only) with S110 (opportunity register in use). |
| 0:08 to 0:15 | Question | Each person writes one risk (first color) and one opportunity (second color) at their station. |
| 0:15 to 0:20 | Action | The supervisor adds the notes to the register and names an owner for one opportunity. |

**Quick check.** Is "a trial of an improved measuring tool that could reduce rework" a risk or an opportunity? *Answer: an opportunity.*

## Session 28: Planning a change: who needs to know

**Objective.** Each participant can name who must be informed before a change at their station, and how the result is checked.

**Materials.** Change Planning Record handout; scenarios S105 and S112.

| Time | Step | Script and notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:03 | Idea | "A change is planned before it is made, the people affected are told, the result is monitored, and someone checks later that it worked. The 2026 edition is reported to strengthen this (6.3, High). In operations it links to 8.5.6." |
| 0:03 to 0:08 | Example | Compare S105 (supplier change, no follow-up) with S112 (planned change done well). |
| 0:08 to 0:15 | Question | Pick a recent or likely change at the station (new tool, new software version, new provider). "Who needs to know? What would we measure? When would we check?" Fill in fields 6 to 8 of the record together. |
| 0:15 to 0:20 | Action | Agree who owns the communication list for the next change. |

> **Best estimate for 8.5.6.** Intent unchanged, possibly with an amended note consistent with stronger change planning. Reasoning: Clause 8 changes are described as minor, and 8.5.6 is the operational counterpart of 6.3. Check: read 8.5.6 and the notes under 8.1 in the purchased text.

**Quick check.** Name the three things beyond approval that planning of a change includes. *Answer: communication to affected people, monitoring, and a review of whether the change achieved its intended result.*
