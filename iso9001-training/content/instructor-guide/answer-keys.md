# Answer Keys: Quick Checks, Modules 0 to 10

*This material is an original training aid. It is not a substitute for the standard. Obtain ISO 9001 from ISO or a national standards body.*

Clause numbers follow ISO 9001:2015. For open questions, accept any answer that contains the key idea and relates it to the participant's own work. Items marked **[OWNER INPUT]** depend on {{COMPANY}} practice.

## Module 0: Orientation

1. **Which clauses contain the auditable requirements?** Clauses 4 to 10. Clauses 1 to 3 give scope, references, and terms as background.
2. **Does ISO 9001 define product or service specifications?** No. It sets requirements for the management system. Customers and the company define the specifications.
3. **Who generates most QMS records?** Frontline staff. They record the work as it happens, and auditors rely on those records as evidence.
4. **What does the Check step of Plan, Do, Check, Act refer to?** Evaluating performance, which is the subject of Clause 9: monitoring, measurement, analysis, internal audit, and management review.

## Module 1: Context and Scope (Clause 4)

1. **Name two interested parties and one need of each.** Example: customers need conforming output on time; regulators need compliance with applicable rules. Accept other valid pairs such as employees (safe conditions) and suppliers (clear requirements).
2. **What does a process owner do?** The process owner is accountable for how the process performs and keeps it under control, including inputs, outputs, resources, measures, and risks.
3. **Why does scope matter at an audit?** It defines what the auditor may examine and states which requirements, if any, do not apply and why.
4. **Give one internal issue and one external issue that could affect your work.** Accept realistic pairs. Internal examples: equipment age, staffing levels, skills. External examples: customer demand, supplier delivery, regulation, technology.

## Module 2: Leadership and Policy (Clause 5)

1. **Who is accountable for QMS effectiveness?** Top management. It may delegate tasks but not accountability.
2. **State the policy in your own words.** Accept any accurate summary of the company's policy commitments (meeting requirements, improving, and what that means at the participant's station). Wording need not match the policy. **[OWNER INPUT]**
3. **Do you have authority to stop a process that produces nonconforming product?** Yes. Everyone is expected to stop work when nonconforming output is being produced and to report it. Confirm the local rule. If your authority is limited, call your supervisor at once and keep the work item on hold. **[OWNER INPUT]**
4. **Give one way your role affects the customer.** Accept any realistic link, such as correct recording, careful handling, timely response, or reporting a defect before it is delivered.

## Module 3: Planning, Risk, and Change (Clause 6)

1. **Give one risk at your station and one control.** Example: measuring tool drift, controlled by calibration and a status check before use. Accept any realistic pair.
2. **Which objective does your station influence?** Example: first-time-right rate, on-time delivery, rework rate. Match it to the current objectives. **[OWNER INPUT]**
3. **What must happen before a process parameter is changed?** The change is reviewed and authorized, with its consequences, effect on the system, resources, and responsibilities considered, and the change is recorded.
4. **Does ISO 9001 require a formal risk management method?** No. Actions must be proportionate to the possible impact. A simple list with owners and actions is enough if it is used.
5. **Why must staff be told about a change before it begins?** People who do not know about a change keep using the old method, which mixes practices and produces nonconforming output. They also need training and a chance to raise problems.

## Module 4: Support (Clause 7)

1. **How do you verify a work instruction is current?** Check its revision identifier against the controlled list or system, and confirm it is the copy issued for use at the point of use.
2. **What do you do when a measuring instrument or test tool is overdue for calibration or verification?** Do not use it. Flag it, and report it to the supervisor so that the validity of earlier results can be assessed.
3. **Why is training evidence retained?** It proves that people are competent for their tasks, and auditors and the company rely on it.
4. **Why is backfilling a record a problem?** A record completed later from memory may be inaccurate, so it cannot serve as objective evidence. It also hides what actually happened at the time.

## Module 5: Operation, Part A (Clauses 8.1 to 8.4)

1. **Who reviews a changed customer requirement before work continues?** The person or function assigned this authority, typically customer service or sales with technical input, before commitment to the changed requirement. Work continues only after review and notification of affected staff. **[OWNER INPUT]**
2. **What is verified at receipt of an externally provided input?** That it matches the specified requirements, such as identity, quantity, condition, version, and required documents.
3. **Give an example of an external provider that is not a supplier of materials.** Examples: a calibration laboratory, a transport company, a cloud service, a subcontractor, or temporary staff.
4. **Why does the company confirm a verbal requirement before starting work?** An undocumented requirement is easily misunderstood. Confirming it before commitment prevents disputes and wasted work.

## Module 6: Operation, Part B (Clauses 8.5 to 8.6)

1. **Why are customer-supplied material and data treated as customer property?** They belong to the customer, so the company must identify, protect, and safeguard them, and report any loss or damage.
2. **What must be recorded when a work item changes status?** The new status, the date and time, who made the change, and the identification of the work item.
3. **Who may authorize release of a work item with an open discrepancy?** Only a person with defined release authority and, where applicable, the customer. Release is not automatic and a record is kept.
4. **What does traceability allow the company to do?** Follow a work item back to its inputs, equipment, and people, and forward to the customers who received it, so that a problem can be contained to the work items affected.

## Module 7: Nonconforming Outputs (Clause 8.7)

1. **What are the four steps?** STOP, FLAG, HOLD, REPORT.
2. **Who dispositions a held work item?** The person or group with documented disposition authority, normally the supervisor with quality. The operator does not decide.
3. **What if the same work item has already been delivered?** Report it at once. The company assesses the effect, decides whether to inform the customer, and takes action such as recall, replacement, or correction.
4. **Why must a corrected output be checked again?** A correction may not have removed the nonconformity, or may have caused a new one. Verification shows that the output now conforms.
5. **Why is quiet rework a problem even when the result is good?** There is no record, no authorized disposition, and no signal that something is wrong, so the cause is never found and the problem returns.

## Module 8: Performance Evaluation (Clause 9)

1. **Why must internal auditors be impartial?** An auditor who examines their own work cannot judge it fairly, so impartiality keeps the results credible.
2. **Name three management review inputs.** Any three of: status of previous actions, changes in context, customer satisfaction, objectives achieved, process performance, nonconformities and corrective actions, monitoring results, audit results, supplier performance, resource adequacy, effectiveness of risk actions, improvement opportunities.
3. **What happens to an audit finding?** It is reported to the relevant management, corrected, and its cause is addressed by corrective action without undue delay, with records retained.
4. **Give two ways the company learns what customers think.** Any two of: complaints, satisfaction surveys, supplier scorecards from customers, direct feedback, returns, compliments, customer meetings.

## Module 9: Improvement and Corrective Action (Clause 10)

1. **Define correction and corrective action.** Correction fixes the nonconforming output. Corrective action removes the cause so that it does not recur.
2. **Complete a 5 Whys for the problem given in the module (a delivery went to the wrong address).** Model answer for a work item that failed a final check. (1) Why did it fail? The cleaning step was skipped. (2) Why was it skipped? The instruction does not state when cleaning is due. (3) Why not? The instruction was revised without review. (4) Why no review? The change process had no review step. (5) Why? The process was never defined. Root cause: an undefined change step. For the wrong-address example, accept a chain such as: the label was wrong because the address was typed by hand; hand entry was used because the order system could not import the address; the import step was never configured; no one reviewed the order process after it changed. Accept any chain that ends in a system condition rather than "operator error".
3. **How do you verify effectiveness?** Review results after a set period, such as three months, to confirm the problem has not recurred, and record the outcome.
4. **Why is "operator error" not enough as a root cause?** It describes the symptom. The useful question is why the system allowed the error: instruction, training, tooling, workload, or design.

## Module 10: Audits: What to Expect

1. **Name three types of audit.** Any three of: internal audit, customer audit, certification (third-party) audit, surveillance audit, recertification audit.
2. **What should you say if you do not know the answer?** Say that you will find out, and tell your supervisor. Do not guess.
3. **Why is a record stronger evidence than a statement?** A record was made at the time of the work and can be checked by someone else. A statement depends on memory and opinion. Auditors sample and rely on evidence.
4. **Who answers a question about another department?** Refer the auditor to your supervisor or the owner of that area. Do not guess or speak for another area.
