# Module Facilitation Notes

*This material is an original training aid. It is not a substitute for the standard. Obtain ISO 9001 from ISO or a national standards body.*

These notes support delivery of Modules 0 to 12 of the {{COMPANY}} participant guide. Each module follows the same nine-item template:

1. Timing (lecture, discussion, activity)
2. Key messages (three)
3. Facilitation script (opening question, transition statements, closing summary)
4. Discussion prompts with expected responses
5. Misconceptions to correct
6. Examples to adapt (fictional, with a field for real examples)
7. Quick check answers with explanations
8. Time-saving options
9. Extension options

Timing is given for Format C in full and summarized for Formats A and B. Clause numbers follow ISO 9001:2015. Items marked **[OWNER INPUT]** need company information. Real examples may be used only with the approval of the area supervisor and quality, and without names of people or customers.

## Module 0: Orientation

### 1. Timing

| Format | Lecture | Discussion | Activity | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 8 | 4 | 3 (Clause Sprint warm-up) | 15 |
| B | 10 | 6 | 4 | 20 |
| C | 12 | 8 | 5 | 25 |

### 2. Key messages

- The QMS is how {{COMPANY}} delivers consistent, conforming output every day, not a set of files for audit day.
- ISO 9001 sets requirements for the management system. Customers and the company set the product and service specifications.
- Frontline staff create most of the evidence an auditor sees, so every record matters.

### 3. Facilitation script

- **Opening question.** "If {{COMPANY}} lost its certificate tomorrow, who would notice first, and why?"
- **Transition to the standard.** "The certificate rests on a standard. Here is how that standard is organized."
- **Transition to roles.** "The structure is abstract until we see where your work fits. Let us find your place in Plan, Do, Check, Act."
- **Closing summary.** "The QMS is the way we work. Clauses 4 to 10 hold the requirements. Your records are the evidence. In the next module we look at who leads the system and what the policy means for you."

### 4. Discussion prompts

| Prompt | Expected responses |
|---|---|
| What does a customer gain from our certificate? | Confidence that the system is audited independently; fewer customer audits; a condition of some contracts. |
| Which records did you make in your last shift? | Check sheets, logs, tickets, signatures, status changes, delivery receipts. Each is QMS evidence. |
| Where does your work sit in Plan, Do, Check, Act? | Most frontline work is Do; checks and measurements are Check; reporting problems feeds Act. |
| Which of the seven principles do you see here most often? | Any principle with a concrete local example. Probe for evidence rather than opinion. |

### 5. Misconceptions to correct

- "Quality is the quality department's job." Everyone who touches a work item affects quality.
- "The standard tells us how to make our product." It sets system requirements only.
- "A certificate means nothing will go wrong." It means the system is audited and able to detect and correct problems.

### 6. Examples to adapt

- Fictional: at Meridian Group, an operator's check sheet was the only evidence that a batch was measured before release. The auditor sampled it.
- Fictional: a support analyst's ticket notes showed the cause of a recurring fault, which led to a corrective action.
- Real example from {{COMPANY}}: ______________________ (approved by: ____________)

### 7. Quick check answers

1. **Which clauses of ISO 9001 contain the auditable requirements?** Clauses 4 to 10. Clauses 1 to 3 give scope, references, and terms as background.
2. **Does ISO 9001 define product or service specifications?** No. Customers and the company define them. The standard covers the system that delivers them.
3. **Who generates most of the records in a QMS?** Frontline staff, because they record the work as it happens.
4. **What does the Check step of Plan, Do, Check, Act refer to?** Evaluating performance (Clause 9): monitoring, measurement, analysis, internal audit, and management review.

### 8. Time-saving options

- Drop the seven principles discussion. Show the list only.
- Skip the Clause Sprint warm-up.

### 9. Extension options

- Ask each table to map three of its own records to a clause.
- Discuss how customers and certification bodies use certificates in purchasing decisions.
- Point to the 2026 Bridge box and Module 11 for the status of the new edition.

## Module 1: Context and Scope (Clause 4)

### 1. Timing

| Format | Lecture | Discussion | Activity | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Not taught | | | 0 |
| B | 8 | 4 | 8 (process map) | 20 |
| C | 12 | 10 | 18 (process map and interested parties) | 40 |

### 2. Key messages

- {{COMPANY}} looks at its internal and external situation because it affects whether outputs conform.
- Interested parties have needs the company tracks when they are relevant to the QMS.
- Every process has inputs, outputs, an owner, resources, measures, and risks. The scope states what the QMS covers.

### 3. Facilitation script

- **Opening question.** "Name one thing outside this building that could stop us from delivering on time next month."
- **Transition to interested parties.** "Those outside pressures come from people and organizations. The standard calls them interested parties."
- **Transition to processes.** "Each party's needs reach us through our processes. Let us draw yours."
- **Closing summary.** "Context tells us what could help or hurt us. Interested parties tell us whose needs count. The scope says what the system covers, and the process map shows how work flows. Next we look at who leads it."

### 4. Discussion prompts

| Prompt | Expected responses |
|---|---|
| Who depends on your output? | The next step, the customer, regulators, colleagues on the next shift. |
| What do you need from the step before yours? | Correct, identified, complete inputs on time, with records. |
| Who owns your process, and how would you know? | A named owner on the process map or organization chart. If no one can answer, that is a gap. |
| What would an auditor ask about our scope? | Which sites, products, and services are covered, and why any requirement does not apply. |

### 5. Misconceptions to correct

- "Context is a management exercise with no link to my work." Equipment age, staffing, and supply problems are context and affect every station.
- "Each step is independent." Steps are linked. A weak output at one step becomes a weak input at the next.
- "A process without a measure is fine if people are experienced." Without a measure, no one can show the process is under control.

### 6. Examples to adapt

- Fictional: Meridian Group's external issue is a single supplier for a key input; the internal issue is an aging bottleneck machine.
- Fictional: a process map for a service desk shows ticket intake, triage, resolution, and closure, each with an owner and a measure.
- Real example from {{COMPANY}}: ______________________ (approved by: ____________)

### 7. Quick check answers

1. **Name two interested parties and one need of each.** Example: customers need conforming output on time; regulators need compliance. Accept other valid pairs.
2. **What does a process owner do?** Is accountable for the process's performance and keeps its inputs, outputs, resources, measures, and risks under control.
3. **Why does the scope matter at an audit?** It defines what the auditor may examine and states which requirements do not apply and why.
4. **Give one internal issue and one external issue that could affect your work.** Internal: equipment age, staffing, skills. External: customer demand, supplier delivery, regulation, technology.

### 8. Time-saving options

- Replace individual process maps with one shared map drawn by the facilitator.
- Cover interested parties through the quick check only.

### 9. Extension options

- Each table maps the handoffs between two adjacent processes and names one requirement the next step needs.
- Review the actual scope statement of {{COMPANY}} and discuss any exclusions. **[OWNER INPUT]**

## Module 2: Leadership and Policy (Clause 5)

### 1. Timing

| Format | Lecture | Discussion | Activity | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 10 | 10 | 5 (policy in own words) | 25 |
| B | 8 | 8 | 4 | 20 |
| C | 12 | 15 | 13 (policy and authority map) | 40 |

### 2. Key messages

- Top management is accountable for the QMS. It may delegate tasks but not accountability.
- The policy matters only when people can say what it means for their own work.
- Everyone must know which authorities they hold, especially the authority to stop work or place a hold.

### 3. Facilitation script

- **Opening question.** "If you saw a problem on your line right now, who could stop the work?"
- **Transition to the policy.** "Authority comes from leadership, and leadership sets direction through the quality policy."
- **Transition to roles.** "A policy needs people with clear roles to carry it out. Let us map who decides what."
- **Closing summary.** "Leaders are accountable. The policy sets the direction. You know what it means at your station and who has authority to stop work. Next we plan how to meet our objectives."

### 4. Discussion prompts

| Prompt | Expected responses |
|---|---|
| What does the policy ask of you on a normal shift? | A concrete action: checking identification, following the current instruction, reporting defects. |
| What stops people from using their authority to stop work? | Schedule pressure, fear of blame, unclear rules. Each points to a leadership action. |
| How does a leader show that raising a problem is welcome? | Thanks the person, acts, gives feedback, does not blame. |
| How does your role affect the customer? | Through accuracy, care, timeliness, and reporting before delivery. |

### 5. Misconceptions to correct

- "I need to memorize the policy." Understanding and application matter, not recitation.
- "Leadership duties belong to senior managers only." Supervisors lead their own areas.
- "Stopping work needs permission first." Confirm the local rule. **[OWNER INPUT]** stop-work authority.

### 6. Examples to adapt

- Fictional: a Meridian Group nurse explains that the policy means checking the patient identifier every time.
- Fictional: a shift leader holds a batch at the end of the shift despite a delivery deadline and is thanked by the plant manager.
- Real example from {{COMPANY}}: ______________________ (approved by: ____________)

### 7. Quick check answers

1. **Who is accountable for the effectiveness of the QMS?** Top management.
2. **State the quality policy in your own words.** Accept any accurate summary linked to the participant's station. **[OWNER INPUT]** policy text.
3. **Do you have authority to stop a process that produces nonconforming output? If not, whom do you call?** Confirm the local rule. Where authority is limited, call the supervisor at once and keep the work item on hold. **[OWNER INPUT]**
4. **Give one way your role affects the customer.** Accept any realistic link, such as correct recording, careful handling, or reporting a defect before delivery.

### 8. Time-saving options

- Show the policy and ask two participants to restate it. Skip the authority map.

### 9. Extension options

- Supervisors draft a two-minute talk explaining the policy to a new starter.
- Review a recent decision in which schedule and quality conflicted, and identify who held the authority.

## Module 3: Planning, Risk, and Change (Clause 6)

### 1. Timing

| Format | Lecture | Discussion | Activity | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Not taught | | | 0 |
| B | 15 | 10 | 15 (risk pairs and change scenario) | 40 |
| C | 18 | 12 | 20 (risk pairs, objective link, change scenario) | 50 |

### 2. Key messages

- Risk-based thinking means considering what could go wrong, or go better, before acting, and acting in proportion.
- Objectives are measurable, and each station can name the objective it influences.
- A change is planned before it starts: purpose, consequences, effect on the system, resources, and responsibilities.

### 3. Facilitation script

- **Opening question.** "What is the one thing at your station most likely to cause a defect next week?"
- **Transition to objectives.** "We manage risks so that we can meet our objectives. Which objectives does your work move?"
- **Transition to change.** "Many risks arrive with a change. Let us look at how a change is planned."
- **Closing summary.** "Identify risks and opportunities, act in proportion, know your objective, and never change a process without review. Next we look at the resources that support the work."

### 4. Discussion prompts

| Prompt | Expected responses |
|---|---|
| Give a risk and a control at your station. | Instrument drift and calibration; input variation and receiving verification; turnover and training. |
| Give an opportunity at your station. | A simpler layout, a better check, a shared lesson from another shift. |
| Which objective does your station influence, and how do you see the result? | First-time-right, on-time delivery, rework rate, response time; results on a board or report. **[OWNER INPUT]** |
| What counts as a change that needs review? | New provider, adjusted setting, new tool or software version, revised template, changed handoff. |
| Why tell staff before a change starts? | So that they do not mix old and new methods, and so they can be trained and raise problems. |

### 5. Misconceptions to correct

- "Risk management needs a complex method." ISO 9001 does not require a formal method. A simple list with owners and actions is enough if it is used.
- "A small adjustment is not a change." If it can affect conformity, it is a change.
- "Objectives are for managers." Every station contributes to at least one objective.

### 6. Examples to adapt

- Fictional: Meridian Group changes a cleaning agent without review; residue causes a test failure two weeks later.
- Fictional: a team adds a second check at a bottleneck as an opportunity and cuts rework by a third.
- Real example from {{COMPANY}}: ______________________ (approved by: ____________)

### 7. Quick check answers

1. **Give one risk at your station and one control for it.** Example: measuring tool drift controlled by calibration and a status check before use.
2. **Which objective does your station influence?** Match it to current objectives. **[OWNER INPUT]**
3. **What must happen before a process parameter is changed?** Review and authorization, considering consequences, effect on the system, resources, and responsibilities, with a record.
4. **Does ISO 9001 require a formal risk management method?** No. Actions must be proportionate to the potential impact.
5. **Why must staff be told about a change before it begins?** To avoid mixed methods and nonconforming output, and to allow training and feedback.

### 8. Time-saving options

- Do the risk pairs as a whole-group list on a flip chart.
- Cover change through quick check question 3 only.

### 9. Extension options

- Run the Change Control Challenge.
- Review an extract of the risk register and assess whether actions are proportionate. **[OWNER INPUT]**
- Discuss how climate change is considered as an issue under the 2024 amendment for {{COMPANY}}. **[VERIFY]**

## Module 4: Support (Clause 7)

### 1. Timing

| Format | Lecture | Discussion | Activity | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 15 | 7 | 8 (revision check and calibration labels) | 30 |
| B | 15 | 5 | 30 (Document Control Relay and debrief) | 50 |
| C | 20 | 10 | 30 (Document Control Relay and debrief) | 60 |

### 2. Key messages

- Use only the current revision at the point of use. Remove obsolete copies.
- Do not use a measuring or test resource whose calibration or verification is overdue. Flag it and report it.
- Record at the time of the activity. Never backfill. Do not perform a task you are not qualified for.

### 3. Facilitation script

- **Opening question.** "How do you know the instruction in front of you is the right one?"
- **Transition to measurement.** "A correct instruction is not enough if the result is measured with a faulty tool."
- **Transition to competence and records.** "Correct instructions and tools still need a competent person who records truthfully."
- **Closing summary.** "Current document, valid measuring resource, competent person, true record made at the time. These four protect every output. Next we look at how work is planned and supplied."

### 4. Discussion prompts

| Prompt | Expected responses |
|---|---|
| Where do personal notes or old copies hide in your area? | Drawers, desktops, wall posts, saved files, photographs on phones. |
| What do you do with an overdue calibration label? | Do not use the resource; flag it; report it so earlier results can be assessed. |
| Why is a record made later from memory a problem? | It may be wrong, and it is no longer objective evidence. |
| How would you know you are qualified for a task? | Training matrix, sign-off, supervisor confirmation. |
| What conditions in your work environment affect quality? | Temperature, cleanliness, lighting, noise, workload, respectful communication. |

### 5. Misconceptions to correct

- "My notes are clearer than the instruction, so they are fine." Uncontrolled notes drift from the current method. Propose a change to the instruction instead.
- "The tool still reads correctly, so the date does not matter." Without valid status there is no evidence that it reads correctly.
- "Filling in the log at the end of the shift is the same." It is not. The record must be made at the time.

### 6. Examples to adapt

- Fictional: at Meridian Group a photocopy of revision C was used after revision D changed a torque value.
- Fictional: a scale found out of tolerance triggers a review of all weighings since its last valid check.
- Real example from {{COMPANY}}: ______________________ (approved by: ____________)

### 7. Quick check answers

1. **How do you verify that a work instruction is current?** Check its revision identifier against the controlled list or system and confirm it is the issued copy at the point of use.
2. **What do you do when a measuring tool or test script is overdue for calibration or verification?** Do not use it. Flag it and report it so that earlier results can be assessed.
3. **Why is training evidence retained?** It shows that people are competent for their tasks.
4. **Why is backfilling a record a problem?** It may be inaccurate, it is not objective evidence, and it hides what happened.

### 8. Time-saving options

- Replace the Relay with a 5-minute demonstration: show two revisions and ask which is current.
- Cover organizational knowledge and communication by reading the key concepts only.

### 9. Extension options

- Technicians review a calibration record and decide what to do with results since an out-of-tolerance finding.
- Supervisors review a training matrix extract and identify gaps and evidence of effectiveness.

## Module 5: Operation, Part A (Clauses 8.1 to 8.4)

### 1. Timing

| Format | Lecture | Discussion | Activity | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Not taught | | | 0 |
| B | 15 | 5 | 5 | 25 (of the shared 50 with Module 6) |
| C | 15 | 10 | 10 (requirement review exercise) | 35 |

### 2. Key messages

- Work is planned with criteria, resources, and controls before it starts.
- Customer requirements are reviewed before the company commits, and changes are communicated to the people affected.
- External providers are controlled in proportion to their effect, and what they supply is verified.

### 3. Facilitation script

- **Opening question.** "What happens here when a customer changes an order after work has started?"
- **Transition to design.** "Some requirements need new products, services, or processes. That is design and development."
- **Transition to external providers.** "Much of what we deliver depends on what others supply to us."
- **Closing summary.** "Plan the work, review requirements before committing, control design where it applies, and verify what providers send. Next we follow the work item through production and release."

### 4. Discussion prompts

| Prompt | Expected responses |
|---|---|
| What do you check when an input arrives? | Identity, quantity, condition, version, required documents. |
| Which external providers are not material suppliers? | Calibration laboratory, transport, cloud service, subcontractor, temporary staff. |
| Why confirm a verbal requirement? | To prevent misunderstanding and wasted work. |
| What happens if a provider changes its process without telling us? | Unexpected variation; the company needs change notification requirements and verification. |

### 5. Misconceptions to correct

- "Receiving is a count, not a check." Receiving verifies conformity to requirements.
- "Only material suppliers are external providers." Services and outsourced processes are included.
- "Design does not apply to us." Confirm the scope. **[OWNER INPUT]** whether design is excluded.

### 6. Examples to adapt

- Fictional: Meridian Group accepts an order with a tighter tolerance than its process can hold, without review.
- Fictional: a cloud provider updates software and breaks a report template used for release.
- Real example from {{COMPANY}}: ______________________ (approved by: ____________)

### 7. Quick check answers

1. **Who reviews a changed customer requirement before work continues?** The person or function with that authority, with technical input, before commitment; affected staff are informed. **[OWNER INPUT]**
2. **What is verified when an externally provided input is received?** That it meets specified requirements: identity, quantity, condition, version, and documents.
3. **Give one example of an external provider that does not supply materials.** A calibration laboratory, transport company, cloud service, subcontractor, or temporary staff agency.
4. **Why does the company confirm a verbal requirement before starting work?** Undocumented requirements are easily misunderstood; confirmation prevents disputes and waste.

### 8. Time-saving options

- Teach design and development by one example only.
- Combine with Module 6 using one work item followed from order to release.

### 9. Extension options

## Module 6: Operation, Part B (Clauses 8.5 to 8.6)

### 1. Timing

| Format | Lecture | Discussion | Activity | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 15 | 7 | 8 (trace a work item) | 30 |
| B | 15 | 5 | 5 | 25 (of the shared 50 with Module 5) |
| C | 20 | 10 | 10 (plus Traceability Challenge, 40 minutes, separate slot) | 40 |

### 2. Key messages

- Every work item is identified and its status is visible at every step.
- Customer property, physical or intangible, is identified, protected, and never mixed.
- Release happens only when planned checks are complete, unless a person with authority, and the customer where needed, approves otherwise.

### 3. Facilitation script

- **Opening question.** "If a customer called about one delivered work item, how fast could we find everything else made the same way?"
- **Transition to customer property.** "Some of what we handle is not ours. Customer property needs extra care."
- **Transition to release.** "Every step ends at release. Release is a decision, with a name on it."
- **Closing summary.** "Identify, mark status, protect, preserve, and release only with authority. Next we deal with the work item that fails."

### 4. Discussion prompts

| Prompt | Expected responses |
|---|---|
| How is status shown on your work items? | Tags, labels, location, system status, color with text. |
| What counts as customer property here? | Customer material, equipment, data, records, specifications, personal information. |
| What do you record when status changes? | New status, date and time, who, work item identification. |
| What pressures lead to skipped checks before release? | Deadlines, staff shortages, assumptions. The answer is escalation, not skipping. |

### 5. Misconceptions to correct

- "Customer property is only physical." Data, records, intellectual property, and personal information are included.
- "If the work item looks right, it can go." Release follows completed checks and authorization.
- "Relabeling is harmless if the item is the same." Merging or relabeling without a record breaks traceability.

### 6. Examples to adapt

- Fictional: at Meridian Group two customers' parts share a bin and are shipped to the wrong customer.
- Fictional: a client file is emailed to the wrong recipient; the company reports the loss to the client.
- Real example from {{COMPANY}}: ______________________ (approved by: ____________)

### 7. Quick check answers

1. **Why are customer-supplied material and data treated as customer property?** They belong to the customer, so the company must identify, protect, and safeguard them and report any loss or damage.
2. **What must be recorded when a work item changes status?** The new status, date and time, who made the change, and the work item identification.
3. **Who may authorize release of a work item with an open discrepancy?** Only a person with defined release authority and, where applicable, the customer, with a record.
4. **What does traceability allow the company to do?** Trace back to inputs, equipment, and people, and forward to customers, so that problems are contained to the affected work items.

### 8. Time-saving options

- Cover preservation and post-delivery activities by reading the key concepts only.
- Use one traced work item as the sole activity.

### 9. Extension options

- Run the Traceability Challenge.
- Discuss process validation where output cannot be fully verified afterwards.

## Module 7: Nonconforming Outputs (Clause 8.7)

This module is the model for all other modules.

### 1. Timing

| Format | Lecture | Discussion | Activity | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 7 | 5 | 8 (hold tag practice; includes 5 minutes on Module 9) | 20 |
| B | 4 | 3 | 3 | 10 (of the shared 30 with Module 9) |
| C | 10 | 10 | 10 (STOP, FLAG, HOLD, REPORT practice) | 30 |

### 2. Key messages

- Speed of containment is the primary protection of the customer.
- STOP, FLAG, HOLD, REPORT, in that order, every time.
- No redo, rework, or scrap without a documented disposition by a person with authority.

### 3. Facilitation script

- **Opening question.** "A work item is about to be handed to the customer (shipped, handed over, or released). You notice a recorded result outside the limit. What do you do first?"
- **Expected answers.** Stop the release; flag and hold the work item; notify the supervisor. Redirect any answer that begins with re-measuring alone, redoing the work without disposition, or checking informally with a colleague.
- **Transition to the four steps.** "What you just described has a name here: STOP, FLAG, HOLD, REPORT."
- **Transition to disposition.** "Once the item is held, someone with authority decides what happens to it, and that decision is recorded."
- **Closing summary.** "Contain first, report at once, and let the authorized person decide the disposition. Next we look at how to stop the problem from coming back."

### 4. Discussion prompts

| Prompt | Expected responses |
|---|---|
| What makes it easy or hard to report here? | Easy: quick response, thanks, clear contact. Hard: fear of blame, schedule pressure, unclear hold area. |
| What does a good flag show? | What is wrong, the date, the name of the person, the work item identification. |
| What if the item has already left? | Report at once; the company assesses effect, decides on informing the customer, and acts. |
| Why is quiet rework harmful even when the result is good? | No record, no authorized disposition, no signal, so the cause is never found. |

### 5. Misconceptions to correct

- "Reporting a problem gets the operator in trouble." Correct this with the company's non-punitive reporting stance. **[OWNER INPUT]** confirm policy.
- "Re-measuring until it passes is fine." Repeated measurement without a documented reason hides a nonconformity.
- "A small defect does not need a record." Every nonconformity is recorded.

### 6. Examples to adapt

- Fictional: a Meridian Group machinist tags an oversized part and holds the batch.
- Fictional: a developer blocks a release after a failed test; a driver quarantines a damaged pallet.
- Real example from {{COMPANY}}: ______________________ (approved by: ____________)

### 7. Quick check answers

1. **What are the four steps, in order?** STOP, FLAG, HOLD, REPORT.
2. **Who decides the disposition of a held work item?** The person or group with documented disposition authority, normally the supervisor with quality. The operator does not decide.
3. **What if the same work item has already been delivered?** Report at once. The company assesses the effect, decides whether to inform the customer, and takes action such as recall, replacement, or correction.
4. **Why must a corrected output be checked again?** The correction may not have worked or may have caused a new problem. Verification shows it now conforms.
5. **Why is quiet rework a problem even when the result is good?** No record, no authorized disposition, and no signal, so the cause is never found.

### 8. Time-saving options

- Drop the concession discussion. Keep STOP, FLAG, HOLD, REPORT.

### 9. Extension options

- Discuss when a customer must be informed and who decides.
- Technicians define the extent of a problem: which other work items share the cause.

## Module 8: Performance Evaluation (Clause 9)

### 1. Timing

| Format | Lecture | Discussion | Activity | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Not taught | | | 0 |
| B | 5 | 5 | 0 (covered in the Quality Flow debrief) | 10 |
| C | 12 | 8 | 5 (plus Management Review Boardroom, 45 minutes, separate slot) | 25 |

### 2. Key messages

- Data is collected to be used: analyzed, discussed, and acted on.
- Internal audit is a planned, impartial check that helps the company find problems before customers do.
- Management review produces decisions, actions, owners, and dates.

### 3. Facilitation script

- **Opening question.** "Which chart in your area has changed a decision in the last month?"
- **Transition to internal audit.** "Charts show results. Internal audits check whether the system behind the results works."
- **Transition to management review.** "Results and audit findings go to top management, who decide what to change."
- **Closing summary.** "Measure, analyze, audit, review, decide. Next we turn those decisions into improvement."

### 4. Discussion prompts

| Prompt | Expected responses |
|---|---|
| Which data at your station is collected but not used? | Any honest example; follow with who should use it. |
| How does the company learn what customers think? | Complaints, surveys, scorecards, returns, direct feedback, compliments. |
| Why can an auditor not audit their own work? | They cannot judge it impartially. |
| What makes a management review output useful? | A decision with an owner, a date, and resources. |

### 5. Misconceptions to correct

- "Internal audit is a formality before the real audit." It is the company's own tool for finding gaps.
- "Management review is a meeting." It is a review with recorded decisions and actions.
- "No complaints means customers are satisfied." Silence is not evidence. Customer perception is monitored actively.

### 6. Examples to adapt

- Fictional: Meridian Group's rework chart rises for three weeks before anyone asks why.
- Fictional: an internal auditor from logistics audits the laboratory and finds an overdue verification.
- Real example from {{COMPANY}}: ______________________ (approved by: ____________)

### 7. Quick check answers

1. **Why must internal auditors be impartial?** Impartiality keeps the results credible; an auditor cannot fairly judge their own work.
2. **Name three management review inputs.** Any three of: status of previous actions, changes in context, customer satisfaction, objectives, process performance, nonconformities and corrective actions, monitoring results, audit results, supplier performance, resource adequacy, effectiveness of risk actions, improvement opportunities.
3. **What happens to an audit finding?** It is reported to relevant management and addressed by correction and corrective action without undue delay, with records retained.
4. **Give two ways the company learns what customers think.** Any two of: complaints, surveys, customer scorecards, feedback, returns, compliments, meetings.

### 8. Time-saving options

- Teach Module 8 only through the Quality Flow debrief: treat the end-of-game data as management review inputs.

### 9. Extension options

- Run the Management Review Boardroom.
- Supervisors draft an internal audit plan for one process, with criteria and scope.

## Module 9: Improvement and Corrective Action (Clause 10)

### 1. Timing

| Format | Lecture | Discussion | Activity | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 3 | 2 | 0 | 5 (within the Module 7 slot) |
| B | 5 | 2 | 13 (Root Cause Relay) | 20 (of the shared 30 with Module 7) |
| C | 10 | 10 | 15 (5 Whys and fishbone practice) | 35 |

### 2. Key messages

- A correction fixes the output. A corrective action removes the cause.
- Root cause is a system condition. Ask why the system allowed the error.
- An action is closed only after its effectiveness is verified.

### 3. Facilitation script

- **Opening question.** "A defect was found, the item was fixed, and the person was retrained. Will it happen again?"
- **Transition to root cause.** "To know whether it will happen again, we need the cause, not just the event."
- **Transition to effectiveness.** "An action is only as good as its result. How do we know it worked?"
- **Closing summary.** "Correct the output, find the system cause, act on it, and check later that the problem has not returned. Next we look at how auditors examine all of this."

### 4. Discussion prompts

| Prompt | Expected responses |
|---|---|
| Why is retraining often a weak corrective action? | It does not change the instruction, tool, or design that allowed the error. |
| Where else could the same cause exist? | Other lines, shifts, sites, or similar products. Check them. |
| How long should we wait before checking effectiveness? | Long enough to see the condition recur if the action failed; often about three months. |
| What improvement have you seen here that lasted? | Any example with a system change behind it. |

### 5. Misconceptions to correct

- "Operator error is a root cause." It describes a symptom.
- "Closing the action is the end." Effectiveness is verified before closure.
- "Improvement only follows problems." Analysis and management review also identify opportunities.

### 6. Examples to adapt

- Fictional: a Meridian Group work item fails a final check because the cleaning interval is not defined. The instruction is revised and staff retrained. Results are reviewed after three months.
- Fictional: a delivery goes to the wrong address because the order system cannot import addresses.
- Real example from {{COMPANY}}: ______________________ (approved by: ____________)

### 7. Quick check answers

1. **Define correction and corrective action.** Correction fixes the nonconforming output. Corrective action removes the cause so it does not recur.
2. **Complete a 5 Whys for this problem: a delivery went to the wrong address.** Accept a chain such as: the label was wrong; the address was typed by hand; the order system could not import the address; the import step was never configured; no one reviewed the order process after it changed. The chain must end in a system condition.
3. **How do you verify effectiveness?** Review results after a set period to confirm the problem has not recurred, and record the outcome.
4. **Why is "operator error" not enough as a root cause?** It names the symptom. The useful question is why the system allowed the error.

### 8. Time-saving options

- Model one 5 Whys on the board. Skip the fishbone.

### 9. Extension options

- Run the Root Cause Relay with evidence cards that include red herrings.
- Use Pareto analysis on a set of nonconformance data to choose which problem to tackle first.

## Module 10: Audits: What to Expect

### 1. Timing

| Format | Lecture | Discussion | Activity | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 4 | 3 | 3 (audit response card practice) | 10 |
| B | 5 | 3 | 2 | 10 |
| C | 8 | 6 | 6 (plus Audit Day, 75 minutes, separate slot) | 20 |

### 2. Key messages

- Auditors sample the system, not the person.
- Answer the question asked, truthfully, and show the evidence.
- If you do not know, say you will find out, and tell your supervisor.

### 3. Facilitation script

- **Opening question.** "What worries people most when an auditor walks into their area?"
- **Transition to the audit process.** "Knowing the steps of an audit removes much of that worry."
- **Transition to response guidelines.** "The process is predictable. Your part is to answer well."
- **Closing summary.** "Audits test the system. Be truthful, show records, do not guess, and refer questions about other areas. Findings are a chance to improve."

### 4. Discussion prompts

| Prompt | Expected responses |
|---|---|
| Why do auditors ask to see records rather than accept answers? | Records are objective and can be checked; statements depend on memory. |
| What should you do if you disagree with a finding? | State the point calmly and let the supervisor follow up. Do not argue. |
| What harm comes from offering extra stories? | They open new lines of questioning outside the sample. |
| What is the difference between a finding and an observation? | A finding is a nonconformity against a requirement; an observation is a suggestion. Confirm terms with the certification body. **[VERIFY]** |

### 5. Misconceptions to correct

- "The audit is a test of me." It is a test of the system.
- "Saying I do not know looks bad." Guessing is worse. "I will find out" is the correct answer.
- "Tidying records before the auditor arrives helps." Altering or hiding records is never acceptable.

### 6. Examples to adapt

- Fictional: an auditor asks a Meridian Group warehouse worker where the latest procedure is kept; the worker opens the controlled system and shows the revision.
- Fictional: an auditor asks a developer to show approval of a recent change.
- Real example from {{COMPANY}}: ______________________ (approved by: ____________)

### 7. Quick check answers

1. **Name three types of audit.** Any three of: internal, customer, certification, surveillance, recertification.
2. **What should you say if you do not know the answer?** "I will find out," then tell the supervisor. Do not guess.
3. **Why is a record stronger evidence than a statement?** It was made at the time and can be checked by someone else.
4. **Who answers a question about another department?** Refer the auditor to your supervisor or the owner of that area.

### 8. Time-saving options

- Read the audit response card aloud and practice one question in pairs.

### 9. Extension options

- Run Audit Day.
- Review the last external audit report and discuss how each finding was closed. **[OWNER INPUT]**

## Module 12: Capstone

### 1. Timing

| Format | Lecture | Discussion | Activity | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A, B | Knowledge check and practical check only (see the assessment guide) | | | |
| C | 0 | 0 | 30 (Part 1) + 30 (Part 2) + 10 per participant (Part 3) | 60 plus Part 3 |

The Quality Flow clause-mapping debrief and the Workplace Evidence Hunt may also be run as part of the capstone.

### 2. Key messages

- The capstone checks application, not recall.
- Contain first, then report, then name the clause, then prevent recurrence.
- A participant who does not pass receives coaching and retakes the parts not yet passed.

### 3. Facilitation script

- **Opening statement.** "This assessment shows that you can apply what you learned. It has three parts. You need 80 percent on the quiz and a satisfactory result on the scenarios and the demonstration."
- **Transition to Part 2.** "Now apply the same knowledge to situations. For each one, write the issue, the first action, the clause, and the preventive action."
- **Transition to Part 3.** "Finally, show us. Use the mock work item as if it were real."
- **Closing summary.** "Results go on your training record. If any part needs another attempt, we will arrange coaching first."

### 4. Discussion prompts

Use these after scoring, not during the assessment.

| Prompt | Expected responses |
|---|---|
| Which scenario was hardest, and why? | Usually the one with competing pressures, such as a delivery deadline. |
| Which immediate actions did the group agree on? | Containment before investigation in every case. |
| Which Quality Flow event would you most like to prevent at work, and which control would do it? | Any event with the matching control and clause. |

### 5. Misconceptions to correct

- "The clause number is the most important answer." The immediate action carries the most weight.
- "Retraining the person is a preventive action." It is acceptable only when combined with a system change.

### 6. Examples to adapt

- Use the scenario sets supplied for the capstone, adapted to the industry pack.
- Real example from {{COMPANY}} for a scenario (anonymized): ______________________ (approved by: ____________)

### 7. Quick check answers

Module 12 has no quick check. Scoring rules:

- **Part 1.** Pass at 80 percent unless the owner sets another value. **[OWNER INPUT]**
- **Part 2.** Satisfactory when the response names the correct issue, an immediate action that contains the problem first, a relevant clause, and a preventive action aimed at the cause rather than at a person.
- **Part 3.** Satisfactory when all steps of STOP, FLAG, HOLD, REPORT and the document currency check are performed in the correct order without prompting.

### 8. Time-saving options

- Run Part 3 on the floor within two weeks rather than in the classroom.

### 9. Extension options

- Participants write one scenario from their own area for the scenario library, with an answer, for review by quality.
