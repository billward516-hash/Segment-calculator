## 1. The sector at a glance

Professional services deliver engagements, client files, reports, analyses, and courses. The work item is the engagement, client file, or course. Quality depends on agreed scope, current templates and data, verified calculation models, peer review before release, protection of confidential client information, competent staff and assessors, and feedback that is used.

**Process map (Meridian Advisory).** Enquiry and scoping → Planning → Delivery or analysis → Peer review → Client sign-off → Aftercare and feedback. The work item is the **engagement**. The constraint stage, where work most often queues, is delivery or analysis.

| Stage | Typical inputs | Typical outputs | Typical owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Enquiry and scoping | Client enquiry, needs, terms | Engagement letter or course agreement with scope | Engagement partner or programme lead |
| 2. Planning | Scope, templates, data sources, models | Engagement plan or course plan | Engagement manager |
| 3. Delivery or analysis | Plan, data, models, templates | Draft deliverable or delivered course | Analysts or trainers |
| 4. Peer review | Draft, review checklist | Reviewed deliverable with review record | Independent reviewer |
| 5. Client sign-off | Reviewed deliverable | Client acceptance or sign-off | Engagement partner |
| 6. Aftercare and feedback | Delivered work, feedback, complaints | Feedback analysis and aftercare actions | Client service lead |

The engagement file index and the data source log are the first records to read in a records trail: they show which template, data version, and model version were used.

## 4. Typical nonconformities and their usual causes

Each likely cause is a system condition, not a person. When a cause names a person ("careless", "forgot"), ask what in the system allowed the event and what would prevent it for anyone in that role.

| No. | Nonconformity | Clause | Likely root cause (system condition) | Preventive control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Deliverable sent without peer review | 8.6 | Status can be set to final without review | Lock final status until review recorded |
| 2 | Outdated report template in use | 7.5.3 | Old templates remain available | Archive superseded templates |
| 3 | Model changed without verification | 7.1.5, 8.5.6 | No model change control | Change request with verification |
| 4 | Client folder open to all staff | 8.5.3 | Folders created with open defaults | Restricted default permissions |
| 5 | Superseded data table used | 7.5.3 | Old tables not marked obsolete | Archive and mark obsolete data |
| 6 | Assumption taken from a personal spreadsheet | 7.5.3 | No rule on data sources | Use the assumptions register only |
| 7 | Scope change without client agreement | 8.2.4 | Changes agreed verbally | Written scope change |
| 8 | Summary figures do not match tables | 8.5.1 | Figures typed by hand | Linked figures |
| 9 | Late changes after review not re-reviewed | 8.5.6 | Workflow allows edits after review | Re-review trigger |
| 10 | Client originals held without return | 8.5.3 | No return process | Return process with register |
| 11 | Learner feedback not acted on | 9.1.2 | No owner for feedback | Owner and review cycle |

## 5. Sector specifics

### Customer property

Client property includes original documents, confidential information, personal data, and assessment materials. Restrict access, record originals, and return them as agreed.

### External providers

Subcontracted specialists, data providers, venues, and software platforms. Evaluate them and verify the data and services they supply.

### Measuring and monitoring resources

Review checklists, assessment rubrics, validated calculation models, and feedback surveys. A model is a measuring resource: changes need verification before use.

### Work environment

Workload, confidentiality of the workspace, remote working, and a respectful climate.
