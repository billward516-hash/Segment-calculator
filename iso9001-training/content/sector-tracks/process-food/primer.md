## 1. The sector at a glance

Process industries make product in batches: sauces, cereal bars, cleaning liquids, cosmetics. Ingredients are weighed, processed, packed, labeled, checked, and released. Quality depends on batch traceability from ingredient lot to customer, release against results, correct label artwork, verified probes and scales, controlled recipes, and protected packaging.

**Process map (Meridian Foods).** Receiving and ingredient check → Preparation and weighing → Cooking or processing → Packaging and labeling → Final check and release → Dispatch. The work item is the **production batch**. The constraint stage, where work most often queues, is cooking or processing.

| Stage | Typical inputs | Typical outputs | Typical owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Receiving and ingredient check | Ingredients, packaging, supplier certificates, purchase orders | Accepted lots with internal codes | Goods-in team |
| 2. Preparation and weighing | Accepted ingredients, recipe sheet, verified scales | Weighed batches with weighing records | Weighing operator |
| 3. Cooking or processing | Weighed batches, process settings, verified probes | Processed product with batch record | Process operator |
| 4. Packaging and labeling | Product, packaging, approved artwork, labeler settings | Packed and labeled cases | Packing line lead |
| 5. Final check and release | Batch record, laboratory results, release checklist | Released batch or held batch | Release technologist |
| 6. Dispatch | Released pallets, dispatch plan, carrier booking | Dispatched orders with batch numbers | Dispatch lead |

Traceability runs forward (ingredient lot to customer) and backward (customer complaint to ingredient lot). Rework and split lots are where the trail most often breaks.

## 4. Typical nonconformities and their usual causes

Each likely cause is a system condition, not a person. When a cause names a person ("careless", "forgot"), ask what in the system allowed the event and what would prevent it for anyone in that role.

| No. | Nonconformity | Clause | Likely root cause (system condition) | Preventive control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Old label artwork revision on the line | 7.5.3 | Artwork changes are not checked at start-up | Artwork revision in the start-up checklist |
| 2 | Batch released before the laboratory result | 8.6 | Release box can be ticked with a blank result | Required result field before release |
| 3 | Ingredient lot not recorded in weighing | 8.5.2 | Lot code is an optional field | Mandatory lot code with scanning |
| 4 | Rework added to a later batch without trace | 8.5.2 | Rework log does not record destination cases | Rework log links to receiving batch and cases |
| 5 | Scale daily check not done | 7.1.5 | No stop point if the check is missing | Scale locked until check recorded |
| 6 | Recipe sheet out of date at the scale | 7.5.3 | Printed copies not withdrawn | Controlled issue and withdrawal |
| 7 | Certificate stamped without comparison | 8.4 | Receiving check is a stamp only | Checklist comparing grade and specification |
| 8 | Customer packaging stored outside and damaged | 8.5.3 | No defined storage for customer property | Designated dry storage and damage reporting |
| 9 | Supplier change notice filed without review | 8.4, 8.5.6 | Notices go to purchasing only | Route notices to technical review |
| 10 | Mock trace overdue | 9.1.1 | Plan has no owner | Named owner and calendar |
| 11 | Recurring label error closed with retraining | 10.2 | Corrective action accepts training as default | Cause prompts and effectiveness check |

## 5. Sector specifics

### Customer property

Customer property includes customer-supplied packaging, private label artwork, customer recipes and specifications, and returnable pallets. It must be identified, stored suitably, and the customer told of damage or loss.

### External providers

External providers include ingredient and packaging suppliers, laboratory testing services, pest control contractors, and temperature-controlled transport. Certificates are compared with specifications, and supplier change notices are reviewed.

### Measuring and monitoring resources

Temperature probes, scales, pH meters, metal detectors, and label verification cameras. Each is verified at a planned interval; when one fails, earlier results are assessed. A camera that checks only the barcode will not detect a wrong date format.

### Work environment

Hygiene zones, temperature, humidity, and segregation set by the food safety system. ISO 9001 asks that the environment needed for conformity is maintained; the food safety requirements themselves are outside this program.

## 6. Sector vocabulary

| Term | Meaning in this primer |
|---|---|
| Production batch | A quantity made together under one batch record; the work item |
| Lot | A quantity of an ingredient or packaging with one identity |
| Lot code | The identity printed or written on a lot |
| Batch record | The record of how a batch was made |
| Recipe sheet | The controlled document listing ingredients and quantities |
| Artwork | The approved design of a label or carton |
| Artwork revision | The version of the artwork |
| Certificate of analysis | A supplier document with test results for a lot |
| Specification | The agreed requirements for an ingredient, packaging, or product |
| Release | The decision that a batch may leave |
| Hold | A status that stops a batch from moving on |
| Rework | Product reprocessed into another batch |
| Mock trace | An exercise to test traceability |
| Best-before date | The date printed for quality, set by the organization |
| Start-up checklist | Checks done before a line starts |
| Label verification | A check that the label printed is correct |
| Private label | Product sold under the customer's brand |
| Yield | Output compared with input |
| Viscosity | How thick a liquid is; a common product characteristic |
| Cold chain | Temperature-controlled storage and transport |
| Shelf-life testing | Testing that the product stays within quality limits over time |
| Disposition | The decision on what to do with held product |

## 7. Sector schemes and local rules

Food businesses often operate a food safety management system and may be certified to food safety schemes; food law applies. Cosmetics and other consumer goods may have their own rules. This program covers the ISO 9001 quality management system only. Where a scheme or legal requirement applies, the organization remains responsible for it; consult the certification body and the relevant authority. **[VERIFY]**
