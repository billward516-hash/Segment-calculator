# Appendix A: Bridging ISO 9001:2015 to ISO 9001:2026

*This material is an original training aid. It is not a substitute for the standard. Obtain ISO 9001 from ISO or a national standards body.*

## A.1 Purpose and use

This appendix bridges the program from the 2015 edition, which is the current certified baseline for {{COMPANY}}, to the 2026 edition. It is the source for Module 11, the 2026 Bridge boxes, the bridging sessions, the gap assessment, and the clause map (`data/clause-map.json`).

**Source status.** This appendix was compiled from secondary commentary (an accreditation-related professional institute, certification bodies, and consultancies) retrieved on 4 October 2026. It was not prepared from the standard. Every statement about the 2026 edition carries a confidence label. Requirement wording is paraphrased, never quoted. Only clause numbers and titles are cited. Lettered sub-items within Clause 5 are not cited, because sources disagree on the lettering.

| Label | Meaning |

According to the commentary reviewed:

- The structure of Clauses 4 to 10, the process approach, and Plan, Do, Check, Act logic remain. (High)
- Risk-based thinking remains a principle. (High)
- The core operational controls carry forward in intent: planning and control, requirements review, external provider control, controlled production, identification and traceability, customer property, preservation, release, and control of nonconforming outputs. (Unclear: sources differ on how much Clause 8 wording changed. **[VERIFY]**)

- No new management system is needed. Existing systems are refined, not replaced. (High)

## A.5 Deep dives

Each deep dive gives the reported change, its plain-language meaning, workplace examples, evidence to prepare, and discussion prompts. Examples are fictional.

### A.5.1 Quality culture and ethical behavior (5.1.1, 7.3, 7.1.4, 4.1)

**Reported change.** Top management promotes and demonstrates a quality culture and ethical behavior (5.1.1, High). Awareness extends to culture and ethics for everyone (7.3, High). The context and working environment clauses acknowledge the influence of culture (4.1, Medium; 7.1.4, Medium).

**Plain-language meaning.**

- *Quality culture* means the shared values, attitudes, and habits that decide how people act on quality, particularly when no one is checking.
- *Ethical behavior* means honesty and integrity in records, reporting, decisions, and dealings with customers and suppliers, including under schedule or cost pressure.
- The visible responsibility sits with leaders. Operators are not asked to adopt personal beliefs. The company creates conditions in which the right action is also the easy one.

**On our floor.**

| Situation | Conforming behavior | Behavior that signals a weak culture |
|---|---|---|
| Out-of-limit result near the delivery deadline | The work item is stopped and held; the supervisor thanks the person and informs the customer if needed | Staff told to re-check until the result passes |
| Reading slightly outside its limit | The actual value is recorded and escalated | The target value is recorded to keep the record clean |
| Delivery pressure with an open discrepancy | The supervisor refuses release and escalates | Release "just this once" |
| Near-miss observed | Reported through a defined channel without fear of blame | Not reported because earlier reporters were reprimanded |
| Leadership visit | The leader asks about problems and obstacles and acts on them | The leader asks only about output |

**Evidence trail (light and practical; Best estimate: auditors are expected to look at behavior and records, not attitudes).**

- Leadership communications that refer to quality and ethics.
- A short ethics or conduct statement in the quality manual or code of conduct. **[OWNER INPUT]** coordinate with Human Resources.
- Records of reports and stop-work events, and how they were handled and recognized.
- Awareness training content and attendance (7.3).
- Management review minutes that discuss culture and behavior, not only metrics.

**Cautions.** Commentary indicates no separate culture program is required (Best estimate; check 5.1.1 and 7.5). Do not create audit criteria that the standard does not state. Auditing culture means examining behavior, decisions, and evidence. Ethics content is reviewed by Human Resources or Legal before delivery; this training gives no disciplinary or legal guidance.

**Role view.**

| Role | What it means |
|---|---|
| Operator | Record accurately, report promptly, and use the stop-work authority |
| Technician | Record true values, escalate excursions, and avoid shortcuts during maintenance and calibration |
| Supervisor | Respond constructively to reports, protect people who speak up, and hold release criteria under pressure |
| Top management | Demonstrate, communicate, and review culture and ethics, with evidence |

**Discussion prompts.**

1. Describe a situation where doing the right thing about quality was difficult. What would have made it easier?
2. What happens here when someone reports a problem that delays a shipment?
3. What would an auditor see or hear that shows leaders take quality seriously?

### A.5.2 Risks and opportunities (6.1.1 to 6.1.3)

**Reported change (High).** Determining and acting on risks and opportunities is separated into distinct subclauses, with expanded guidance. The subclause numbers are reported, not verified.

**Plain-language meaning.** Risks could prevent intended results. Opportunities could improve results. The company identifies both, decides which matter, plans proportionate actions, and checks whether the actions worked. A formal risk methodology remains non-mandatory (Best estimate: no source reports a new method requirement; check 6.1.1 to 6.1.3). **[VERIFY]**

| Type | Item | Action | Owner | Check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Risk | Input variation from an external provider | Input verification, provider agreement | Supplier quality | First-pass rate |
| Risk | Only one qualified person for a key method | Cross-training plan | Supervisor | Training matrix |
| Opportunity | Improved measuring or automation tools could reduce rework | Business case and trial | Engineering or process owner | Rework rate |
| Opportunity | Customer interest in a new or extended service | Capability study | Operations | Qualified orders |

**Training implication.** Every risk exercise gets an opportunity column. Operators contribute improvement ideas; supervisors manage the register.

**Discussion prompts.** What risk would you name at your station? What opportunity could you name?

### A.5.3 Planning of changes (6.3, linked to 8.5.6)

**Reported change (High).** Planning of QMS changes is reinforced with expectations for communication, monitoring, evaluation, and review so that changes deliver their intended results.

**Plain-language meaning.** A change is planned before it is made. Affected people are informed. Results are monitored. The company checks later whether the change worked and adjusts if it did not.

**Change Planning Record fields** (see the handout):

1. Description and purpose of the change
2. Potential consequences and risks
3. Effect on QMS integrity (documents, training, records, calibration)
4. Resources required
5. Responsibilities and approvals
6. Communication plan (who needs to know, by when)
7. Monitoring plan (what will be measured and for how long)
8. Review date and effectiveness decision
9. Records retained

**In practice.** A new external provider, an adjusted process setting, a new measuring tool or software version, a changed handoff method, or revised packaging or templates. In operations, 8.5.6 (control of changes) is the linked clause (Best estimate: intent unchanged; check 8.5.6).

### A.5.4 Context, climate, and strategic alignment (4.1, 4.2, 5.2.1)

**Reported change.** Climate change relevance is written into the context clauses (High; first introduced by Amendment 1:2024). The quality policy is reported to align with strategic direction (Single).

**Plain-language meaning.** The company decides whether climate change is a relevant issue for its QMS and whether interested parties have climate-related requirements. If it is relevant, the company plans for it.

**Possible considerations (fictional, to be tailored).** Reliability of power, water, or network supply; extreme weather affecting sites or logistics; environmental, chemical, or waste handling requirements where relevant; customer sustainability requests; and energy cost. **[OWNER INPUT]** decide which, if any, are relevant.

**Evidence.** Updated context and interested party registers showing the decision, with a short rationale whether or not the issue is judged relevant.

### A.5.5 Organizational knowledge (7.1.6)

**Reported change (Medium).** Organizational knowledge is treated more broadly and tied to intended results.

**Plain-language meaning.** The company identifies the knowledge its processes depend on and keeps it available: how to run the process, why parameters were chosen, and what was learned from problems.

**On our floor.** Lessons-learned entries after corrective actions; cross-training; structured shift handoff; capture of expertise before retirements or role changes; process notes linked to instructions.

**Evidence.** Lessons-learned log, training matrix with backup coverage, handoff records.

### A.5.6 Continual improvement (10.1)

**Reported change.** The two 2015 improvement clauses (10.1 and 10.3) are consolidated into one (Medium; detail Single). Guidance links improvement to changes in context, risks, opportunities, and technology (Single). Some commentary reports a stronger link between leadership commitment and improvement (Unclear). **[VERIFY]**

**Plain-language meaning.** Improvement is deliberate. Inputs include data analysis, audit results, complaints, and management review outputs. Opportunities, not only failures, trigger improvement. Correction and corrective action stay distinct (10.2, no substantive change reported, Medium).

### A.5.7 Annex A

Annex A is informative, not a requirement. It is reported to be expanded across Clauses 4 to 10 (High). Instructors read the Annex A guidance for each affected clause after the standard is purchased, and use it to support plain-language explanations. Annex A text is not copied into training materials.

## A.9 Transition planning checklist

1. Purchase ISO 9001:2026 and, if relevant, the ISO 9000:2026 vocabulary standard.
2. Obtain the certification body's transition plan, audit schedule, and dates.
3. Confirm every item in the verification register (A.10) against the text and update its status.
4. Complete the gap assessment worksheet (A.8).
5. Update the context and interested party registers (climate decision).
6. Add opportunity management to the risk process.
7. Revise the change control procedure to include communication, monitoring, and review.
8. Update the quality manual (if retained), policy, and objectives for alignment with strategy and for culture and ethics.
9. Update awareness and induction training; deliver the bridging sessions (A.7).
10. Revise internal audit checklists; schedule an internal audit against the new requirements.
11. Include the transition in the next management review, with decisions and actions recorded.
12. Retain evidence of the transition plan and its execution.
