# Module 2: Leadership and Policy

*This material is an original training aid. It is not a substitute for the standard. Obtain ISO 9001 from ISO or a national standards body.*

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module, you can:

1. Describe who is accountable for the effectiveness of the QMS.
2. State the quality policy of {{COMPANY}} in your own words.
3. Explain what customer focus means in your role.
4. Identify your own responsibilities and authorities, including authority to stop work.

## Why it matters

A quality system works only when leaders set the direction and people at every level act on it. If leaders give quality low priority, staff soon learn that shortcuts are acceptable. If leaders provide resources, listen to problems, and back the right decision, customers receive consistent outputs.

## Key concepts

**Leadership and commitment (Clause 5.1).** Top management means the person or group that directs and controls {{COMPANY}} at the highest level. It is accountable for how well the QMS works. In practice, top management:

- makes sure the policy and objectives fit the direction of the business;
- provides people, equipment, and time;
- promotes the process approach and risk-based thinking, which means considering what could go wrong or help before acting;
- keeps attention on customer requirements and on risks to conformity;
- supports other managers in leading their own areas.

**Customer focus (Clause 5.1.2).** Leaders make sure that customer and legal requirements are understood and met. They also watch for risks that could harm a customer's result. Customer focus is not limited to the sales team. Anyone who handles a work item, which is the unit of work that moves through a process, affects the customer.

**Quality policy (Clause 5.2).** The quality policy is a short statement of intent from top management. It must suit the purpose of {{COMPANY}}, provide a framework for setting quality objectives, include a commitment to meet applicable requirements, and include a commitment to improve. It must be communicated, understood, and available to relevant interested parties. You do not need to recite the words. You need to explain what the policy means for your work.

**Roles, responsibilities, and authorities (Clause 5.3).** Top management assigns and communicates who does what and who may decide what. It also assigns someone to report on QMS performance and someone to keep the system whole when changes occur. An "authority" is the right to make a decision or take an action.

## What this looks like on our floor

**Frontline staff.** You can explain the policy using an example from your own station. A nurse might say that it means checking a patient identifier every time. A warehouse worker might say that it means scanning every pallet before loading. You know who your supervisor is and whom to call when something is wrong.

**Technical staff.** You know which decisions are yours, such as whether to take an instrument out of use or to approve a test result. You know which decisions need approval from someone else.

**Supervisors.** You know the authority you hold, including the authority to place a work item on hold or to stop a process that is producing nonconforming output. A "hold" is a status that prevents a work item from moving on. It may be a physical tag, an electronic block, or a paused release. Nonconforming means not meeting a requirement. You also show your team that raising a problem is welcome. When a person reports an error, you thank them and act.

## Common mistakes

- Memorizing the policy wording without knowing what it means for your work.
- Unclear authority to stop work, so people wait for permission while problems continue.
- Believing that leadership duties belong only to senior managers.
- Reporting good news only, so leaders see an inaccurate picture.
- Letting schedule pressure override a quality requirement without a decision by someone with authority.

## Quick check

1. Who is accountable for the effectiveness of the QMS?
2. State the quality policy in your own words.
3. Do you have authority to stop a process that produces nonconforming output? If not, whom do you call?
4. Give one way your role affects the customer.
