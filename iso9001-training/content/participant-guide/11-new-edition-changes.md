# Module 11: New Edition Changes

*This material is an original training aid. It is not a substitute for the standard. Obtain ISO 9001 from ISO or a national standards body.*

**Applies to:** Operators (optional), technicians (required), supervisors (required).

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module, each participant can:

1. State the reported status and timeline of ISO 9001:2026 and explain what a transition period means for {{COMPANY}}.
2. List what is reported to stay the same between the 2015 and 2026 editions.
3. Identify the changes that most affect their own role.
4. Explain, in plain language, quality culture and ethical behavior, the separation of risks and opportunities, and the planning of changes.
5. Describe the confidence label attached to each reported change and why it matters.

## Why it matters

{{COMPANY}} is certified against ISO 9001:2015. A new edition has been reported, and the company must prepare before its certification body begins auditing against it. Early preparation spreads the effort over time. Some of the changes, notably the expectation of quality culture and ethical behavior, affect how every person at the company works.

**What a transition period means.** For a defined time, certificates issued against the 2015 edition remain valid. By the end of that time, certified companies must have moved to the new edition. {{COMPANY}} will continue to be audited against the 2015 edition until its certification body states otherwise. The core modules of this program therefore teach the 2015 requirements.

**Decisions that belong to {{COMPANY}} leadership.**

1. The target date for completing the internal bridge. A suggested point is one full internal audit cycle before the first audit against the new edition.
2. Whether to transition at the first available audit or at the end of the transition period.
3. The person responsible for transition planning.

## 2. What stays the same

According to the commentary reviewed, the following carry forward.

- The structure of Clauses 4 to 10 and the process approach with Plan, Do, Check, Act logic. (Confidence: High)
- Risk-based thinking as a principle. (High)
- The core operational controls: planning and control, requirements review, external provider control, controlled production, identification and traceability, customer property, preservation, release, and control of nonconforming outputs. Changes in Clause 8 are reported as limited. (Unclear: sources differ on how much was amended.)
- Internal audit and management review as mechanisms, and corrective action. No substantive change was reported. (Medium)
- No requirement to build a new management system. Existing systems are refined, not replaced. (High)

**What this means in practice.** The skills taught in Modules 0 to 10 remain valid: current documents, accurate records, calibration status, identification, STOP, FLAG, HOLD, REPORT, root-cause analysis, and audit responses.

## 4. Deep dives

### 4.1 Quality culture and ethical behavior (5.1.1, 7.3, 7.1.4, 4.1)

**Reported change (High).** Top management must promote and demonstrate a quality culture and ethical behavior. Awareness requirements extend to everyone. The context and working-environment clauses acknowledge the influence of culture (Medium).

**Plain-language meaning.**

- *Quality culture* is the set of shared values, attitudes, and habits that determine how people behave toward quality, particularly when no one is checking.
- *Ethical behavior* is honesty and integrity in records, reporting, decisions, and dealings with customers and suppliers, including under schedule or cost pressure.
- The requirement places visible responsibility on leaders. It does not ask operators to adopt personal beliefs. It asks the company to create conditions in which the right action is also the easy one.

**What it looks like at {{COMPANY}} (fictional examples).**

| Situation | Conforming behavior | Behavior that signals a weak culture |
|---|---|---|
| Out-of-limit result found near the delivery deadline | Staff stop and hold the work item. The supervisor thanks them and informs the customer if needed. | Staff are told to re-check until the result passes. |
| Reading slightly outside limit | The technician records the actual value and escalates. | The value is recorded as the target to keep the record clean. |
| Delivery pressure with an open discrepancy | The supervisor refuses release and escalates. | Release "just this once". |
| Near-miss observed | Reported through a defined channel without fear of blame. | Staff avoid reporting because earlier reporters were reprimanded. |
| Leadership visit to the workplace | The leader asks about problems and obstacles and acts on them. | The leader asks only about output. |

**Evidence to prepare (light and practical; best estimate).**

- Leadership communications that refer to quality and ethics.
- A short conduct statement within the quality manual or code of conduct. **[OWNER INPUT]** Coordinate with Human Resources.
- Records of reports and stop-work events, and how they were handled and recognized.
- Awareness training content and attendance.
- Management review minutes that discuss culture and behavior, not only metrics.

**Cautions.**

- Commentary indicates no separate culture program is required. Do not create audit criteria that the standard does not state.
- Auditing culture means examining behavior, decisions, and evidence, not attitudes.
- Ethics content must be reviewed by Human Resources or Legal before delivery. Quality training does not give disciplinary or legal guidance.

| Role | What it means |
|---|---|
| Operator | Record accurately, report promptly, and use the authority to stop work. |
| Technician | Record true values, escalate excursions, and resist shortcuts during maintenance and calibration. |
| Supervisor | Respond constructively to reports, protect people who speak up, and hold release criteria under pressure. |
| Top management | Demonstrate, communicate, and review culture and ethics, with evidence. |

**Discussion prompts.**

1. Describe a situation where doing the right thing about quality was difficult. What would have made it easier?
2. What happens here when someone reports a problem that delays a delivery?
3. What would an auditor see or hear that shows leaders take quality seriously?

### 4.2 Risks and opportunities (6.1.1 to 6.1.3)

**Reported change (High).** The determination and treatment of risks and opportunities are separated into distinct subclauses, with expanded guidance.

**Plain-language meaning.** A risk is something that could prevent an intended result. An opportunity is something that could improve a result. The company identifies both, decides which matter, plans proportionate action, and checks whether the action worked. A formal risk management method remains non-mandatory. **[VERIFY]**

**Worked example (fictional).**

| Type | Item | Action | Owner | Check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Risk | Input variation from an external provider | Input verification and a provider agreement | Supplier quality | First-pass rate |
| Risk | One qualified person for a key method | Cross-training plan | Supervisor | Training matrix |
| Opportunity | Better measuring or automation tools could reduce rework | Business case and trial | Engineering or process owner | Rework rate |
| Opportunity | Customer interest in a new or extended service | Capability study | Operations | Qualified orders |

**What to do now.** Add an opportunity column to every risk exercise. Operators contribute improvement ideas. Supervisors manage the register.

**Discussion prompts.** Which risk would you name at your station? Which opportunity?

### 4.3 Planning of changes (6.3, linked to 8.5.6)

**Reported change (High).** Planning of QMS changes is reinforced with expectations for communication, monitoring, evaluation, and review, so that changes deliver the intended results.

**Plain-language meaning.** A change is planned before it is made. Affected people are informed. Results are monitored. The company later checks whether the change worked and adjusts if it did not.

**Change planning record (template fields).**

1. Description and purpose of the change
2. Potential consequences and risks
3. Effect on QMS integrity (documents, training, records, calibration)
4. Resources required
5. Responsibilities and approvals
6. Communication plan (who needs to know, and by when)
7. Monitoring plan (what is measured, and for how long)
8. Review date and effectiveness decision
9. Records retained

**Examples of changes.** A new external provider; an adjusted process setting; a new measuring tool or software version; a changed handoff method; revised packaging or templates.

### 4.4 Context, climate, and strategic alignment (4.1, 4.2, 5.2.1)

**Reported change.** Climate change relevance is written into the context clauses (High). The quality policy and objectives are expected to align with strategic direction (Single).

**Plain-language meaning.** The company decides whether climate change is a relevant issue for its QMS and whether interested parties have climate-related requirements. If it is relevant, the company plans for it.

**Possible considerations (to be tailored).** Reliability of power, water, or network supply; extreme weather affecting sites or logistics; environmental handling requirements where relevant; customer sustainability requests; energy cost. **[OWNER INPUT]** Determine which, if any, are relevant to {{COMPANY}}.

**Evidence to prepare.** An updated context register and interested party register showing the determination, with a short rationale whether or not the issue is judged relevant.

### 4.5 Organizational knowledge (7.1.6)

**Reported change (Medium).** The treatment of organizational knowledge is broader and tied to the intended results of the QMS.

**Plain-language meaning.** The company identifies the knowledge its processes depend on and keeps it available. This covers how to run the process, why parameters were chosen, and what was learned from problems.

**At {{COMPANY}}.** Lessons-learned entries after corrective actions; cross-training; structured shift handoff; capture of expertise before retirements or role changes; process notes linked to instructions.

**Evidence to prepare.** A lessons-learned log, a training matrix with backup coverage, and handoff records.

### 4.6 Continual improvement (10.1)

**Reported change (Medium for consolidation; Single for detail).** The two 2015 improvement clauses are combined into one. Guidance links improvement to changes in context, risks, opportunities, and technology. Some commentary reports a stronger link between leadership commitment and improvement. **[VERIFY]**

**Plain-language meaning.** Improvement is deliberate. Its inputs include data analysis, audit results, complaints, and management review outputs. The distinction between correction and corrective action in Module 9 is unchanged. Opportunities, not only failures, can now trigger improvement.

### 4.7 Annex A and instructor reference

Annex A is informative guidance, not a requirement (High). It is reported to be expanded across Clauses 4 to 10. Instructors should read the Annex A guidance for each affected clause after purchasing the standard. Annex A text is not copied into this program.

## 6. Transition planning checklist (for supervisors and quality)

1. Purchase ISO 9001:2026 and, if relevant, the ISO 9000:2026 vocabulary standard.
2. Obtain the certification body's transition plan, audit schedule, and dates.
3. Confirm every item in this module against the text and update its status.
4. Complete the gap assessment worksheet.
5. Update the context and interested party registers (climate determination).
6. Add opportunity management to the risk process.
7. Revise the change control procedure to include communication, monitoring, and review.
8. Update the quality manual (if retained), policy, and objectives for alignment with strategy and for culture and ethics.
9. Update awareness and induction training. Deliver bridging sessions.
10. Revise internal audit checklists. Schedule an internal audit against the new requirements.
11. Include the transition in the next management review, with decisions and actions recorded.
12. Retain evidence of the transition plan and its execution.
