Part 1: Multiple-choice items

CN-D01. A crew is using drawing revision C; revision D is current. What should happen?

  • A) Continue and fix later
  • B) Ask the client
  • C) Stop that work and issue revision D
  • D) Use both

Answer: C. Current drawings at the work front. (Cl. 7.5.3; F)

CN-D02. A slab is poured before the hold point is signed. What is affected?

  • A) Release of work past a hold point
  • B) Policy
  • C) Context
  • D) Calibration

Answer: A. Hold points must be released first. (Cl. 8.6; F, S)

CN-D03. A subcontractor starts work with no evaluation. What is right?

  • A) Wait until the end
  • B) Ignore it
  • C) Pay them less
  • D) Evaluate and approve before work continues

Answer: D. External providers are evaluated before use. (Cl. 8.4; S)

CN-D04. Cube press verification is overdue. What should happen?

  • A) Continue
  • B) Stop using it until verified and review recent results
  • C) Remove the label
  • D) Use another without checking

Answer: B. Verified test equipment. (Cl. 7.1.5; T)

CN-D05. A pour record does not show which truck went to which column. What is the risk?

  • A) None
  • B) Affected elements cannot be identified if a test fails
  • C) Higher cost
  • D) Faster pours

Answer: B. Traceability of work. (Cl. 8.5.2; T)

CN-D06. The driver adds water to a truck to aid placing and nobody records it. What is missing?

  • A) More water
  • B) Nothing
  • C) A faster pump
  • D) Control and record of changes during placement

Answer: D. Process control includes site additions. (Cl. 8.5.1; T)

CN-D07. Client-supplied fittings are stored open near a wet area. What is required?

  • A) Protect them and record condition; report damage
  • B) Use them anyway
  • C) Return them
  • D) Ignore it

Answer: A. Client property must be protected. (Cl. 8.5.3; F)

CN-D08. Procurement wants to switch panel suppliers to save cost. The contract requires client approval. What first?

  • A) Approve now
  • B) Tell the client later
  • C) Complete evaluation, design review, certificates, and client approval
  • D) Change only some panels

Answer: C. Substitutions follow review and contract terms. (Cl. 8.2.3, 8.4; S)

CN-D09. Anchor errors recur on several projects and are closed as "crew reminded". What is the auditor's question?

  • A) Who the crew was
  • B) Why they were slow
  • C) What shared process lets steel and concrete drawings disagree
  • D) Nothing

Answer: C. Recurring errors point to the system. (Cl. 10.2; S)

CN-D10. Water testing covered 2 of 48 windows; the ITP says 10 percent. What is this?

  • A) Planned verification not completed
  • B) Good practice
  • C) Not relevant
  • D) A design input

Answer: A. Do the verification as planned. (Cl. 8.6; T)

CN-D11. A foreman says "we always get it signed after". Which clause is most affected?

  • A) Calibration
  • B) Context
  • C) Design
  • D) Leadership and commitment at supervisor level

Answer: D. Supervisors support controls. (Cl. 5.1.1; S)

CN-D12. A certificate covers an 18 mm panel; 25 mm is specified. What is the issue?

  • A) None
  • B) Verification evidence does not match the product
  • C) A typing error only
  • D) Packaging

Answer: B. Certificates must match the product. (Cl. 8.4; T)

CN-D13. Changes from the client arrive by phone and are marked up later. What is the risk?

  • A) None
  • B) Crews may build to unrecorded or uncommunicated changes
  • C) Better speed
  • D) Cost only

Answer: B. Changes must be recorded and communicated. (Cl. 8.2.4, 7.4; S)

CN-D14. Nobody owns coordination between steel and concrete drawings. Which clause?

  • A) Calibration
  • B) Customer property
  • C) Policy
  • D) Roles, responsibilities, and authorities

Answer: D. Assign interface responsibility. (Cl. 5.3; S)

CN-D15. After handover the client reports leaks. What should containment include?

  • A) Protection of the affected areas and checks of other projects built the same way
  • B) Nothing; handover was signed
  • C) Only the reported windows
  • D) A discount

Answer: A. Containment covers similar output. (Cl. 8.7; S)

CN-D16. The cube curing tank heater failed for three days. What does this affect?

  • A) Nothing
  • B) Delivery times
  • C) Confidence in test results
  • D) Policy

Answer: C. Test conditions matter. (Cl. 7.1.4; T)

CN-D17. Crew competence records are current, with one faded but legible copy. Is this a finding?

  • A) Yes, major
  • B) Yes, minor
  • C) No, the records are adequate
  • D) Only if photocopied

Answer: C. Legible and current is adequate. (Cl. 7.2; F)

CN-D18. A weather board shows rain. Is this a quality nonconformity?

  • A) No, it is planning context
  • B) Yes
  • C) Yes, minor
  • D) Only if wrong

Answer: A. Not every observation is a finding. (Cl. 7.1.4; F)

1. The sector at a glance

Construction and engineering work is delivered as projects. The work item is a work package or deliverable, such as a slab pour or a window installation. Quality depends on current drawings at the work front, inspection and test plans with hold points, evaluated subcontractors, traceable materials and pours, verified test equipment, and controlled changes from the client and the design team.

Process map (Meridian Build). Tender and requirements review → Design and planning → Site execution → Inspection and testing → Handover review → Close-out and warranty. The work item is the work package. The constraint stage, where work most often queues, is site execution.

5. Sector specifics

Customer property

Client property includes client drawings and models, client-supplied materials and fittings, site access granted by the client, and existing structures. Protect it, record its condition, and report damage.

External providers

Subcontractors, material suppliers, testing laboratories, and plant hire. Evaluate subcontractors before they start, issue them current drawings, and review their material changes.

Measuring and monitoring resources

Survey instruments, concrete and soil test equipment, cube presses, and pressure and torque gauges. Verify them at set intervals and consider test conditions such as curing.

Work environment

Weather, access, lighting, and noise affect work. Site safety is managed separately and is outside this program.

Part 1: Multiple-choice items

DM-D01. A machinist finds drawing revision B at the workstation; the document system shows revision C. What should the machinist do?

  • A) Continue, since the change is probably small
  • B) Ask a colleague which revision they used
  • C) Stop, obtain revision C, and inform the supervisor
  • D) Finish the batch and report afterward

Answer: C. Only the current approved revision may be used at the point of use. (Cl. 7.5.3; F)

DM-D02. A torque wrench label shows calibration expired three weeks ago. What is correct?

  • A) Do not use it; tag it and report it to the calibration owner
  • B) Use it with extra care
  • C) Remove the label
  • D) Borrow another without checking its status

Answer: A. An instrument past its interval cannot be relied on. (Cl. 7.1.5; F)

DM-D03. At calibration, a micrometer is found out of tolerance. What must happen besides adjustment?

  • A) Nothing more
  • B) Replace the label only
  • C) Delete the old results
  • D) Assess the validity of earlier measurements and act on affected product

Answer: D. The organization reviews earlier results when an instrument is found out of tolerance. (Cl. 7.1.5; T)

DM-D04. Two batches of similar parts are mixed and their labels are lost. Which requirement is most affected?

  • A) Awareness
  • B) Identification and traceability
  • C) Organizational context
  • D) Customer satisfaction

Answer: B. Outputs must stay identified and, where required, traceable. (Cl. 8.5.2; F, T)

DM-D05. Bar stock arrives without the certificate required by the order. What is appropriate?

  • A) Machine one part to test it
  • B) Hold the lot and verify the requirement before use
  • C) Accept it and ask later
  • D) Judge it by appearance

Answer: B. External inputs are verified before use. (Cl. 8.4; T)

DM-D06. Engineering wants to raise cutting speed on a critical dimension. What comes first?

  • A) The operator adjusts gradually
  • B) Record it after the next audit
  • C) Tell quality only
  • D) Review consequences, authorize, communicate, and record the change

Answer: D. Production changes are reviewed and controlled. (Cl. 8.5.6; T, S)

DM-D07. Three of 50 parts fail final dimensional check. What must happen before shipping the rest?

  • A) Segregate the failed parts and evaluate whether the others are affected
  • B) Ship 47 with a note
  • C) Re-measure only the three
  • D) Rework three and ship all

Answer: A. Nonconforming output is controlled and release follows verification. (Cl. 8.7, 8.6; F, S)

DM-D08. Customer free-issue components arrive and one is damaged. What is correct?

  • A) Use it if it looks fine
  • B) Scrap it quietly
  • C) Record it, protect the item, and inform the customer
  • D) Replace it from own stock without telling the customer

Answer: C. Damaged customer property is reported to the customer. (Cl. 8.5.3; F)

DM-D09. Who may authorize release of a batch with an open inspection discrepancy?

  • A) Any operator
  • B) The truck driver
  • C) A person with defined authority and, where applicable, the customer
  • D) Nobody, ever

Answer: C. Release without completed arrangements needs relevant authority and, where applicable, the customer. (Cl. 8.6; S)

DM-D10. The same burr recurs and is removed by hand each time. What is missing?

  • A) Investigation of cause, action to remove it, and a check that it worked
  • B) Faster hand deburring
  • C) A stricter final inspection only
  • D) A reminder to operators

Answer: A. Correction deals with the output; corrective action removes the cause. (Cl. 10.2; T)

DM-D11. A machining supervisor audits his own cell. What is the concern?

  • A) The audit is too short
  • B) Audits must be done by customers
  • C) Audits apply only to offices
  • D) Objectivity and impartiality

Answer: D. Internal auditors must be objective and impartial. (Cl. 9.2; S)

DM-D12. A customer tightens a tolerance on an open order. What must happen?

  • A) Continue unchanged
  • B) Review the change, confirm capability, update documents, and inform the people affected
  • C) Inform sales only
  • D) Apply it to the next order

Answer: B. Changed requirements are reviewed and communicated. (Cl. 8.2.4; T, S)

DM-D13. A first-off inspection record shows ticks but no measured values. What is the best response as an auditor?

  • A) Raise a major nonconformity at once
  • B) Record an observation and ask whether values are needed for traceability and trend analysis
  • C) Ignore it
  • D) Ask the inspector to redo every check

Answer: B. Ticks show the check was done; values make it verifiable. Judge against the defined requirement. (Cl. 8.5.1; T)

DM-D14. A gauge is calibrated but uses 35 percent of the tolerance. What does this mean?

  • A) It is fully suitable because it is calibrated
  • B) It must be scrapped
  • C) Calibration intervals must be shortened only
  • D) It may not be suitable for the check even though it is accurate

Answer: D. Suitability for the measurement is separate from calibration. (Cl. 7.1.5; T)

DM-D15. A supplier sends a notice that it is changing an insert grade. What should happen?

  • A) Review the change for its effect before accepting it, and record the decision
  • B) File the notice
  • C) Reject all future deliveries
  • D) Let the setter decide on the day

Answer: A. Changes to externally provided products are evaluated before use. (Cl. 8.4, 8.5.6; T, S)

DM-D16. A shift misses three hours of in-process checks after an equipment fault. What is the system question?

  • A) Who was on shift
  • B) Why the operator was slow
  • C) What reaction plan should apply when equipment fails, and who decides
  • D) How to hide the gap

Answer: C. Controlled production includes defined reactions to process upsets. (Cl. 8.5.1; S)

DM-D17. A shipment target is at risk. A supervisor tells inspectors to sign release before checks are done. Which clause is most affected besides release?

  • A) Calibration
  • B) Infrastructure
  • C) Leadership support for the quality system
  • D) Design inputs

Answer: C. Leaders must not let targets override planned controls. (Cl. 5.1.1; S)

DM-D18. Quality objectives are posted on the shift board with this month's results. What does this show?

  • A) Objectives are communicated and monitored
  • B) A nonconformity because boards are informal
  • C) Nothing relevant
  • D) That the board needs document control

Answer: A. Objectives should be communicated and monitored where staff see them. (Cl. 6.2, 7.3; F)

DM-T01. Which sector example best restates "documented information needed at the point of use must be the current version"?

DM-T02. "Pallet P-31 holds parts from two batches with no separation label." Which general requirement does this restate?

DM-T03. Which sector example restates "the organization reviews the validity of earlier results when an instrument is found out of tolerance"?

  • C) The gauge is moved to a new shelf

DM-T04. "A plating subcontractor changed its process without telling us." Which general requirement does this restate?

  • B) Internal communication
  • C) Organizational knowledge only

DM-T05. Which sector example restates "release of output requires completed planned verification or authorized approval"?

  • B) Packing starts when the truck arrives

5. Sector specifics

Customer property

Customer property includes free-issue components, customer tooling and fixtures, customer drawings and models, and returnable packaging. External providers

External providers include bar stock and casting suppliers, plating and heat-treatment subcontractors, calibration laboratories, tooling suppliers, and freight carriers. Controls range from certificate checks to first-article inspection and subcontractor audits. A change notice from a supplier, such as a new insert grade, is a change to review, not a document to file.

Measuring and monitoring resources

Calipers, micrometers, height gauges, bore gauges, air gauges, coordinate measuring machines, torque tools, and scales. Work environment

Temperature for precision measurement, cleanliness, lighting for inspection, and orderly workstations. Machine guarding and ergonomics are safety matters managed by the safety system; they are outside this program.

1. The sector at a glance

Healthcare organizations deliver services to patients in cases or episodes of care. From a management system view, quality depends on current approved documents and forms, verified measuring devices, complete handover and discharge records, protection of personal information, competent permanent and agency staff, reliable communication with patients and families, and feedback that is analyzed and used.

Process map (Meridian Care). Referral and intake → Assessment → Treatment or procedure → Documentation and results review → Discharge or handover check → Follow-up. The work item is the case. The constraint stage, where work most often queues, is treatment or procedure.

5. Sector specifics

Customer property

Patient property includes personal information, records, personal belongings, and samples. Protect it, give access only to those who need it, and report loss.

External providers

Pharmaceutical and supply vendors, external laboratories, equipment service providers, print suppliers, and agency staff. Verify what they provide and check agency staff competence.

Measuring and monitoring resources

Measuring devices, laboratory instruments, audit tools, and patient feedback surveys. Verify devices at set intervals; the device owner and responsible clinicians decide any clinical review of earlier readings.

Work environment

Privacy, noise, staffing and workload, and infection control. Infection control is a clinical topic outside this program.

5. Sector specifics

Customer property

Customer property includes customer-supplied packaging, private label artwork, customer recipes and specifications, and returnable pallets. It must be identified, stored suitably, and the customer told of damage or loss.

External providers

External providers include ingredient and packaging suppliers, laboratory testing services, pest control contractors, and temperature-controlled transport. Certificates are compared with specifications, and supplier change notices are reviewed.

Measuring and monitoring resources

Temperature probes, scales, pH meters, metal detectors, and label verification cameras. Each is verified at a planned interval; when one fails, earlier results are assessed. A camera that checks only the barcode will not detect a wrong date format.

Work environment

Hygiene zones, temperature, humidity, and segregation set by the food safety system. ISO 9001 asks that the environment needed for conformity is maintained; the food safety requirements themselves are outside this program.

Client property includes original documents, confidential information, personal data, and assessment materials. Restrict access, record originals, and return them as agreed.

External providers

Subcontracted specialists, data providers, venues, and software platforms. Evaluate them and verify the data and services they supply.

Measuring and monitoring resources

Review checklists, assessment rubrics, validated calculation models, and feedback surveys. A model is a measuring resource: changes need verification before use.

Work environment

Workload, confidentiality of the workspace, remote working, and a respectful climate.
