Circle one letter for each question.

  1. A machinist finds that the drawing at the workstation shows revision B. The document system shows revision C was released last week. What should the machinist do?
    • A. Finish the batch and report afterward.
    • B. Continue, since the difference is probably small.
    • C. Ask a colleague which revision they used last.
    • D. Stop, obtain the current revision, and inform the supervisor.
  2. A torque wrench carries a calibration label that expired three weeks ago. What is the correct action?
    • A. Borrow the same model from another area without checking it.
    • B. Do not use it; flag it and report it to the supervisor or calibration owner.
    • C. Remove the label so the issue is not visible.
    • D. Use it and apply a safety margin.
  3. Three of 50 parts fail the final dimensional check. Production wants to ship the other 47. What must happen first?
    • A. Re-measure only the three failed parts.
    • B. Rework the three and ship all 50.
    • C. Ship the 47 with a note to the customer.
    • D. Identify and segregate the failed parts and evaluate whether the remaining parts are affected before release.
  4. A customer sends free-issue components for an assembly job. One arrives damaged. Which action is correct?
    • A. Use the part if it looks usable.
    • B. Record it, protect the item, and inform the customer.
    • C. Replace it from own stock without telling the customer.
    • D. Scrap it quietly.
  5. Two batches of similar parts are mixed in one container and their labels are lost. Which requirement is most directly affected?
    • A. Identification and traceability.
    • B. Awareness.
    • C. Customer satisfaction measurement.
    • D. Organizational context.
  6. A batch has an open inspection discrepancy. Who may authorize release?
    • A. The truck driver.
    • B. A person with defined authority and, where applicable, the customer.
    • C. Nobody; release is never allowed.
    • D. Any operator on the shift.
  7. Bar stock arrives without the material certificate required by the purchase order. What is appropriate?
    • A. Hold the lot and verify the requirement before use.
    • B. Ask the operator to judge the material by appearance.
    • C. Machine one part to test it first.
    • D. Accept it and request the certificate later.
  8. Engineering wants to raise the cutting speed on a process that controls a critical dimension. What should happen before the change?
    • A. Only quality is told.
    • B. The change is recorded after the next audit.
    • C. The change is reviewed for consequences, authorized, communicated, and recorded.
    • D. The operator adjusts it gradually.
  9. At calibration, a micrometer is found out of tolerance. What must the organization do beyond adjusting it?
    • A. Evaluate the validity of earlier measurements and take action on any affected product.
    • B. Delete the old records.
    • C. Replace the label only.
    • D. Nothing further.
  10. The same burr defect recurs and is removed by hand each time. Which element of corrective action is missing?
    • A. Investigation of the cause, action to eliminate it, and a check that it worked.
    • B. A stricter shipping inspection only.
    • C. Faster hand removal.
    • D. A written reminder to operators.
  11. The machining supervisor audits his own department. What is the concern?
    • A. Audits apply only to offices.
    • B. The audit is too short.
    • C. Audits must be done by customers.
    • D. Objectivity and impartiality.
  12. A customer tightens a tolerance on an open order. What must happen?
    • A. Production continues unchanged.
    • B. The next order will reflect it.
    • C. The change is reviewed, the ability to meet it confirmed, documents updated, and relevant people informed.
    • D. Only sales is informed.

Sector quiz: Discrete Manufacturing (Form B)

Meridian Works · 12 questions · 15 minutes · pass mark 80 percent (configurable)

Circle one letter for each question.

  1. A new customer asks Meridian Works to keep inspection records for longer than the current practice. Where should the organization first take account of this expectation?
    • A. Only in the sales contract file.
    • B. Nowhere until the first audit.
    • C. In its review of interested parties and their relevant requirements, and then in the affected processes.
    • D. Only in the operator handbook.
  2. A new deburring cell is added between machining and assembly. What should the process description for the cell show?
    • A. Nothing; small cells do not need a description.
    • B. Its inputs, outputs, sequence, links to other processes, controls, and the person responsible.
    • C. Only the purchase price of the machine.
    • D. Only the name of the operator.
  3. An operator finds a part that may be nonconforming but does not know who can decide what happens to it. What does this indicate?
    • A. The issue concerns the operator's training only.
    • B. The part should be shipped.
    • C. Responsibilities and authorities have not been clearly assigned and communicated.
    • D. The operator should decide alone and tell no one.
  4. During an audit, an assembler cannot explain how her inspection step affects the customer. Which requirement area is weakest?
    • A. Design and development.
    • B. Calibration.
    • C. Customer property.
    • D. Awareness of how one's work contributes to quality and what happens if requirements are not met.
  5. While planning a new product line, engineering finds that a single supplier provides a critical casting. What is the expected planning response?
    • A. Ask operators to work faster.
    • B. Cancel the product line.
    • C. Record the risk, plan proportionate actions such as qualifying a second source, and later check that the actions worked.
    • D. Wait until a supply failure occurs.
  6. A press has a recurring oil leak that the maintenance plan does not cover. What is the most relevant action?
    • A. Run the press until it stops.
    • B. Move the work to a machine that is not approved for it.
    • C. Wipe it each shift and say nothing.
    • D. Report it so the equipment is maintained and the maintenance plan is reviewed.
  7. A shift ends without handing over an open quality hold to the next shift. What is the gap?
    • A. Pricing.
    • B. Internal communication: what is communicated, when, to whom, and how.
    • C. Calibration.
    • D. Policy wording.
  8. Sales wants to accept an order with a delivery date and a tolerance the plant has never achieved. What should happen before the order is accepted?
    • A. Accept it and resolve problems later.
    • B. Review the requirements and confirm the organization can meet them, resolving any differences before commitment.
    • C. Let the customer find out at delivery.
    • D. Accept the delivery date only.
  9. An operator runs a job from memory because there is no work instruction at the station. Which condition for controlled production is missing?
    • A. Customer satisfaction.
    • B. Interested parties.
    • C. Available documented information that defines the work and the results to be achieved.
    • D. Management review.
  10. Finished parts are stored uncovered next to a grinding area. Which requirement is most at risk?
    • A. Internal audit.
    • B. Quality policy.
    • C. Organizational knowledge.
    • D. Preservation of outputs during production and storage.
  11. Scrap data is collected each week but nobody reviews it. What is missing?
    • A. Analysis and evaluation of the data to judge performance and find where to improve.
    • B. A new form.
    • C. Nothing; collecting the data is sufficient.
    • D. More data collection.
  12. An operator suggests a fixture change that would prevent a common setup error. What is the best response?
    • A. Record it, evaluate it through the change process, and give the operator feedback.
    • B. Reject it because operators do not design fixtures.
    • C. Adopt it at once without review.
    • D. Ignore it.
