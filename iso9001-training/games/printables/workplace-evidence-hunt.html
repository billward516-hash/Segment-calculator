Workplace Evidence Hunt: facilitator sheet

Meridian Group. Pairs. 20 to 30 minutes. Modules 4, 6, and 7. Operators and technicians.

Safety rule. Participants observe only. They do not touch equipment, open systems, or enter restricted zones. Areas are visited only with the area supervisor's approval, arranged in advance. Digital areas are viewed with the system owner present.
No blame. Record what is seen, not who did it. Do not write names on the checklist. Gaps are system findings.

Timing

MinuteStep
0 to 3Assign areas and items. Read the safety rule aloud.
3 to 18Pairs observe and record evidence of conformity and gaps.
18 to 25Pairs report back. The group sorts gaps by clause on the report-back sheet.
25 to 30The group selects two gaps for immediate improvement and names an owner for each.

Area assignment

PairArea (physical or digital)Items to checkSupervisor approval (initials)

Scoring (optional)

One point for each item with specific evidence recorded (what was seen, where). One point for each gap stated as a fact without blame. The aim is accurate observation, not the number of gaps.

What to look for, by role and sector

#ItemWhat to look for
1Policy displayed and understandableThe current policy is visible or easy to reach. A person nearby can explain it in their own words.
2Current objectives visible with recent dataObjectives for the area are shown with data from the last period, not last year.
3Instructions, procedures, or templates at point of use are currentCompare the revision at the point of use with the revision in the document system.
4No uncontrolled copies or personal notes in useLook for photocopies, handwritten notes, or saved files used in place of the controlled document.
5Measuring and testing resources show valid statusCalibration or verification labels or system status are in date and legible.
6Training and qualification matrix posted and currentThe matrix shows the people working today as qualified for their tasks.
7Work items show unique identification and statusEach work item has an identifier and a visible status: physical label or system status.
8Hold area or hold status segregated, labeled, and flaggedHeld items are separate, marked, and recorded. Nothing on hold can be used by mistake.
9Customer property identified and protectedCustomer material, equipment, or data is marked and protected from loss or damage.
10Inputs and consumables labeled, controlled, and in date or valid versionLabels show identity and expiry or version. Expired or superseded inputs are removed.
11Environmental or operating conditions recorded and within limitsConditions that matter (temperature, cleanliness, system availability, or similar) are recorded and within limits.
12Records completed at the time, legible, and attributedEntries are made as the work is done, readable, and initialed or attributed to a user.
13Handling, packaging, storage, or handover follow instructionsWork items are handled and stored as the instruction says, and handovers are recorded.
14Nonconformance log current and dispositions assignedOpen entries have an owner and a disposition. Nothing is open without action.
15People can state their authority to stop a processAsk: "If you saw a problem, could you stop the process? Whom would you tell?"

Workplace Evidence Hunt: debrief

Debrief

QuestionExpected answer
Which items had clear evidence of conformity?Accept specific evidence: a label, a revision number, a record entry. Recognize good practice before discussing gaps.
Which gaps were found most often, and which clause do they relate to?Typical groupings: document currency (7.5.3), measuring status (7.1.5), identification and hold status (8.5.2, 8.7). Sorting by clause shows where the system is weak.
How did you record a gap without blame?State what was seen and where, not who. Example: "Instruction at station 3 shows revision C; the system shows revision D."
Why select only two gaps?Two actions with an owner and a date are more likely to be completed than a long list. The remaining gaps go to the area supervisor for the normal process.
What would an auditor ask about these items?To see the evidence, and to ask the person at the workstation to explain it. Item 15 is a common audit question.
