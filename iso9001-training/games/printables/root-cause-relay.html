Root Cause Relay: facilitator sheet

Meridian Group (Generic (sector-neutral)). Teams of 4 to 5. 30 to 40 minutes. Module 9 (Clause 10.2).

Materials per team

  • One problem card (a different problem for each team, or the same problem for every team for comparison).
  • The six evidence cards for that problem, kept by the facilitator and injected in the order shown.
  • One why-chain sheet, one fishbone sheet, and one scoring sheet.

Timing

MinuteStep
0 to 3Read the problem card aloud. Explain the relay: each member in turn adds one why and one because.
3Inject evidence cards 1 and 2.
8Inject evidence cards 3 and 4 (card 4 is relevant).
13Inject evidence cards 5 and 6 (both red herrings in the key).
13 to 20Complete the why chain. Mark any evidence the team rejects and say why.
20 to 27Build the fishbone. Select one root cause.
27 to 32Write the corrective action and the effectiveness check.
32 to 40Score and debrief.

Evidence cards are shuffled on the printed sheet so that red herrings are not always last. Inject them in card number order.

Scoring rules

  • A why counts only if it is a fact, follows from the previous line, and is supported by an evidence card or the problem card.
  • A chain that ends in "the operator made a mistake" stops scoring at that line.
  • An action that only repeats training or reminds people scores 0 for the cause criterion unless it changes the method, the system, or the check.
  • An effectiveness check scores 2 only if it states what is measured, the sample or period, and the pass condition.

Instructor key: problem P1

Repeat defect at the Core process stage. The same defect appears on three consecutive work items leaving the Core process stage. Each was caught at Final check.

Evidence cards

CardStatusText
1RelevantThe set-up record shows that the setting at Core process was adjusted on the night shift. It was not returned to the documented value.
2Red herringThe area was repainted the week before the defects appeared. The paint work did not touch the equipment or the work items.
3RelevantThe work instruction for Core process says "set as required". It gives no value or tolerance.
4RelevantTwo of the three people involved learned the set-up from a colleague. No qualification record exists for the set-up step.
5Red herringOne person returned from leave on the day the first defect appeared. The defect also occurred on a shift when that person was absent.
6RelevantNo check is required after a set-up change. The first sign of the defect came from Final check.

Model why chain

  1. The work item was outside its requirement because the setting at Core process was wrong.
  2. The setting was wrong because it was adjusted on the night shift and not restored.
  3. It was not restored because no documented value exists to restore it to.
  4. No value exists because the instruction was issued without a defined setting and tolerance.
  5. Nothing detected the change because no check is required after a set-up change.
ElementModel answer
Root causeThe setting value and tolerance are not defined in the controlled instruction, and no verification is required after a set-up change.
Corrective actionRevise the instruction to state the setting and tolerance. Require a first-work item check after any set-up change. Qualify everyone who performs the set-up.
Effectiveness checkNo recurrence of the defect in the next 20 work items or four weeks, whichever is longer; a sample of set-up records shows the first-work item check completed every time.

Instructor key: problem P2

Wrong identification on delivered work items. A customer reports that two delivered work items carried the wrong identification. The identifications had been swapped.

Evidence cards

CardStatusText
1RelevantLabels or system identifiers are prepared in advance for the whole shift.
2Red herringThe printer was low on ink that day. All labels were legible.
3RelevantTwo work items changed order after one was held. The pre-prepared identifications were not reassigned.
4RelevantThe Final check checklist asks whether an identification is present. It does not ask whether it matches the record.
5Red herringA new customer service person took the complaint call.
6RelevantThe instruction permits advance preparation of identifications. No risk assessment was made when this was allowed.

Model why chain

  1. The work items carried each other's identification.
  2. The identifications were prepared before the order of work was final.
  3. The order changed after a hold and nobody reassigned them.
  4. The final check confirmed presence, not match to the record.
  5. The method allowing advance preparation was approved without considering a change of order.
ElementModel answer
Root causeIdentification is prepared separately from the work item record, and the final check does not verify the match.
Corrective actionProduce the identification at completion from the record. Add a check at Final check that compares the identification with the record.
Effectiveness checkNo identification mix-ups for three months; a monthly sample of 30 work items shows identification matches the record in every case.

Instructor key: problem P3

A rejected input reached Preparation. An input batch that failed its check at Intake was used at Preparation.

Evidence cards

CardStatusText
1RelevantThe rejected input batch was placed next to accepted stock with a handwritten note. The note was later found on the floor.
2Red herringThe provider used a new format for its delivery paperwork. The check at Intake still detected the failure.
3RelevantThere is no designated hold location or hold status at Intake.
4RelevantThe status in the system was not updated. The person responsible was absent and no deputy was named.
5Red herringThe delivery arrived late in the afternoon. The check was completed on time.
6RelevantThe person at Preparation saw no hold sign and no hold status, and used the input as normal.

Model why chain

  1. A rejected input was used at Preparation.
  2. Nothing showed that it was rejected at the point of use.
  3. The only sign was a loose note, and the system status was not changed.
  4. There is no segregated hold location or hold status.
  5. No deputy is named for status updates when the responsible person is absent.
ElementModel answer
Root causeNo segregated, labeled hold location or system hold exists for rejected inputs, and no deputy covers status updates.
Corrective actionCreate a marked hold location and a system hold that blocks use. Name a deputy for status updates. Brief all staff at Intake and Preparation.
Effectiveness checkWeekly hold area audit for eight weeks finds every rejected input segregated and blocked; no rejected input is used in three months.

Instructor key: problem P4

Release before the final check. A work item was released before the Final check record was signed, to meet a delivery date.

Evidence cards

CardStatusText
1RelevantThe release authorization list names one person. That person was on leave.
2Red herringThe customer had changed its delivery address that month.
3RelevantThe plan for Fridays leaves no time for Final check on the last work items of the week.
4RelevantThe system allows Delivery to proceed when the sign-off field is empty.
5Red herringWeather delayed the vehicle by one hour after release.
6RelevantTwo similar early releases occurred last quarter. Both were treated as one-off events.

Model why chain

  1. The work item was released without sign-off.
  2. Nobody with authority was available and the date was close.
  3. Only one person is authorized and no deputy is named.
  4. The system does not stop release without sign-off.
  5. Earlier early releases were corrected but never investigated for cause.
ElementModel answer
Root causeRelease is not blocked without authorization, only one person is authorized, and planning does not allow time for the final check.
Corrective actionAdd a system block on release without sign-off. Authorize and train deputies. Include final check time in the plan. Investigate repeat events as corrective action.
Effectiveness checkRecord review shows no release without sign-off for three months; planning records show final check time on every Friday.

Root Cause Relay: debrief

Debrief

QuestionExpected answer
Why is "operator error" a weak conclusion?It names a person, not a cause. It does not explain why the system allowed the error, so retraining alone rarely prevents recurrence. Ask why the error was possible and why it was not detected.
What changed in the system after your corrective action?A concrete change to a method, instruction, check, system block, or resource. Reminders and retraining alone do not change the system.
How did you decide that a card was a red herring?The card did not explain the timing or pattern of the problem, or the defect occurred without that factor present.
What is the difference between the correction and the corrective action?The correction deals with the affected work items now (hold, redo, scrap). The corrective action removes the cause so the problem does not recur (Clause 10.2).
How would you know the action worked?A measurable check over a stated period or sample, reviewed by a named person, before the action is closed.
Which requirement does this exercise relate to?Clause 10.2 (nonconformity and corrective action), with links to 8.7 (control of nonconforming outputs) and 9.1.3 (analysis).
