Module 0 Summary: Orientation

This material is an original training aid. It is not a substitute for the standard. Obtain ISO 9001 from ISO or a national standards body.

Meridian Group one-page summary. Applies to: all roles (required).

Key points

  • A quality management system (QMS) is the set of processes, rules, records, and responsibilities that lets Meridian Group deliver consistent, conforming output.
  • ISO 9001 sets requirements for the system. Customers and the company set product and service specifications.
  • Certification means an independent body has audited the system and found it conforms.
  • Clauses 4 to 10 hold the auditable requirements and follow Plan, Do, Check, Act.
  • Frontline staff create most of the evidence an auditor reviews.

Do

  • Know your tasks and follow the current instruction.
  • Record what you did, at the time you did it.
  • Report anything that does not look right.

Do not

  • Treat quality as the quality department's job only.
  • Treat the system as something that exists only for audit day.

Clause reference

Clauses 1 to 3 (background); Clauses 4 to 10 (requirements); 7.3 (awareness).

Quick check

Question. Who generates most of the records in a QMS?

Answer. Frontline staff, because they record the work as it happens.

Module 1 Summary: Context and Scope

This material is an original training aid. It is not a substitute for the standard. Obtain ISO 9001 from ISO or a national standards body.

Meridian Group one-page summary. Applies to: technicians and supervisors (required); operators (optional).

Key points

  • Meridian Group identifies internal and external issues that affect its ability to deliver conforming output, such as demand, technology, supply, workforce, and equipment age.
  • Interested parties (customers, regulators, employees, suppliers, owners) have needs the company tracks when they are relevant.
  • The scope states which products, services, sites, and activities the QMS covers, and justifies any requirement that does not apply.
  • Every process has inputs, outputs, an owner, resources, measures, and risks.

Do

  • Know what your step needs from the step before it, and what the next step needs from you.
  • Know who owns your process.
  • Know how your step is measured.

Do not

  • Treat your step as isolated from the rest of the process.
  • Run a step with no owner or no measure.

Clause reference

4.1, 4.2, 4.3, 4.4

Quick check

Question. Why does the scope matter at an audit?

Answer. It defines what the auditor may examine and states which requirements, if any, do not apply and why.

Meridian Group one-page summary. Applies to: all roles (required).

Key points

  • Top management is accountable for the effectiveness of the QMS. It may delegate tasks but not accountability.
  • Leaders keep attention on customer requirements and on risks to conformity.
  • The quality policy commits Meridian Group to meeting applicable requirements and to improving. It is communicated, understood, and available.

Do

  • Let schedule pressure override a quality requirement without a decision by someone with authority.

5.1, 5.1.2, 5.2, 5.3

Module 3 Summary: Planning, Risk, and Change

This material is an original training aid. It is not a substitute for the standard. Obtain ISO 9001 from ISO or a national standards body.

Meridian Group one-page summary. Applies to: technicians and supervisors (required); operators (optional).

Key points

  • Risk-based thinking means considering what could go wrong, or go better, before acting, and acting in proportion to the possible impact.
  • ISO 9001 does not require a formal risk management method.
  • Quality objectives are measurable, consistent with the policy, monitored, communicated, and updated. OWNER INPUT current objectives.
  • Changes are planned before they start: purpose, consequences, effect on the system, resources, and responsibilities.
  • Since the 2024 amendment, the company determines whether climate change is a relevant issue.

Do

  • Know one risk at your station and the control for it.
  • Know which objective your work influences.
  • Raise a change request before changing a process, tool, input, or method.

Do not

  • Write objectives that cannot be measured.
  • Treat risk as paperwork only.
  • Change a process setting without review and authorization.

Clause reference

6.1, 6.2, 6.3

Quick check

Question. What must happen before a process parameter is changed?

Answer. The change is reviewed and authorized, considering its consequences, effect on the system, resources, and responsibilities, and it is recorded.

Module 4 Summary: Support

This material is an original training aid. It is not a substitute for the standard. Obtain ISO 9001 from ISO or a national standards body.

Meridian Group one-page summary. Applies to: all roles (required).

Key points

  • People, infrastructure, and the work environment are provided and maintained.
  • Measuring and monitoring resources are suitable, calibrated or verified where required, identified for status, and protected. If one is found out of tolerance, earlier results are assessed.
  • People are competent for their tasks, and evidence of competence is retained.
  • People know the policy, their objectives, their contribution, and the effect of not conforming.
  • Documented information is reviewed, approved, identified, and controlled in use, storage, change, and retention.

Do

  • Use only the current revision at the point of use.
  • Check calibration or verification status before using a measuring resource.
  • Record data at the time of the activity, legibly, with initials or user identification and date.
  • Ask before performing a task you are not qualified for.

Do not

  • Keep personal notes or old copies that differ from the controlled instruction.
  • Pre-fill or backfill a record.
  • Use a resource whose calibration or verification is overdue.

Clause reference

7.1.2, 7.1.3, 7.1.4, 7.1.5, 7.1.6, 7.2, 7.3, 7.4, 7.5

Quick check

Question. What do you do when a measuring resource is overdue for calibration or verification?

Answer. Do not use it. Flag it and report it so that earlier results can be assessed.

Module 5 Summary: Operation, Part A

This material is an original training aid. It is not a substitute for the standard. Obtain ISO 9001 from ISO or a national standards body.

Meridian Group one-page summary. Applies to: technicians and supervisors (required); operators (optional).

Key points

  • Work is planned and controlled with criteria, resources, controls, and records. Planned changes are controlled.
  • Customer requirements are determined and reviewed before Meridian Group commits to supply. Changed requirements are communicated to the people affected.
  • Design and development is controlled where it applies. OWNER INPUT whether design is in scope.
  • External providers of materials, services, and processes are evaluated and controlled in proportion to their effect, and what they supply is verified.

Do

  • Check identity, quantity, condition, version, and documents of every input at receipt.
  • Confirm a verbal requirement before starting work.
  • Report a supplier change notice to the supervisor.

Do not

  • Accept an order without reviewing whether it can be met.
  • Use unapproved consumables, tools, or services.
  • Ignore a change notice from a supplier.

Clause reference

8.1, 8.2, 8.3, 8.4

Quick check

Question. Give one example of an external provider that does not supply materials.

Answer. A calibration laboratory, a transport company, a cloud service, a subcontractor, or a temporary staff agency.

Module 6 Summary: Operation, Part B

This material is an original training aid. It is not a substitute for the standard. Obtain ISO 9001 from ISO or a national standards body.

Meridian Group one-page summary. Applies to: all roles (required).

Key points

  • Work is done under controlled conditions: current instructions, suitable equipment and environment, competent people, and planned checks.
  • Every work item is identified, and its status (in process, on hold, released) is visible, physically or electronically.
  • Customer property, physical or intangible, is identified, protected, and safeguarded. Loss or damage is reported.
  • Outputs are preserved through handling, packaging, storage, and transfer.
  • Release happens only when planned checks are complete, unless a person with authority, and the customer where applicable, approves otherwise.

Do

  • Keep identification and status on every work item at every step.
  • Record status changes with date, time, and your identification.
  • Keep each customer's material and data separate.

Do not

  • Merge or relabel work items without a record.
  • Skip a check under time pressure.
  • Release a work item without authority.

Clause reference

8.5.1, 8.5.2, 8.5.3, 8.5.4, 8.5.5, 8.5.6, 8.6

Quick check

Question. Who may authorize release of a work item with an open discrepancy?

Answer. Only a person with defined release authority and, where applicable, the customer, with a record kept.

Module 7 Summary: Nonconforming Outputs

This material is an original training aid. It is not a substitute for the standard. Obtain ISO 9001 from ISO or a national standards body.

Meridian Group one-page summary. Applies to: all roles (required).

Key points

  • A nonconforming output is a product, service, or result that does not meet its requirements.
  • It is identified and controlled so that nobody uses or delivers it by mistake.
  • Speed of containment is the primary protection of the customer.
  • A person with authority decides the disposition. Corrected output is checked again.
  • Records describe the nonconformity, actions, any concession, and who decided.

STOP, FLAG, HOLD, REPORT

  1. STOP the process if the cause is still active.
  2. FLAG the work item and affected material with a tag or electronic status.
  3. HOLD it in a separate location or system status.
  4. REPORT to your supervisor and quality at once.

Do

  • Act without waiting to be asked.
  • Report even small defects.

Do not

  • Rework, redo, or scrap without a documented disposition.
  • Release "just this once".
  • Leave suspect material at the station.

Clause reference

8.7

Quick check

Question. Who decides the disposition of a held work item?

Answer. The person or group with documented disposition authority, normally the supervisor with quality. The operator does not decide.

Module 8 Summary: Performance Evaluation

This material is an original training aid. It is not a substitute for the standard. Obtain ISO 9001 from ISO or a national standards body.

Meridian Group one-page summary. Applies to: technicians and supervisors (required); operators (optional).

Key points

  • Meridian Group decides what to monitor and measure, how, and when to analyze the results.
  • Customer perception is monitored through complaints, surveys, scorecards, and feedback.
  • Internal audits are planned, impartial, and reported to management. Findings are corrected without undue delay.
  • Management review at planned intervals considers performance, audit results, risks, resources, and improvement, and produces decisions and actions.

Do

  • Keep station charts and boards current and use them in team discussions.
  • Help internal auditors by showing records and explaining your work.
  • Follow up actions assigned to you until they are closed.

Do not

  • Collect data that nobody reviews.
  • Treat internal audit as a formality.
  • Hold a review that ends without actions, owners, and dates.

Clause reference

9.1, 9.2, 9.3

Quick check

Question. Why must internal auditors be impartial?

Answer. An auditor cannot fairly judge their own work. Impartiality keeps the results credible.

Module 9 Summary: Improvement and Corrective Action

This material is an original training aid. It is not a substitute for the standard. Obtain ISO 9001 from ISO or a national standards body.

Meridian Group one-page summary. Applies to: all roles (required).

Key points

  • A correction fixes the nonconforming output. A corrective action removes the cause so that it does not recur.
  • When a nonconformity occurs: react, find the cause, check whether similar problems exist, act, and review effectiveness.
  • Root cause is a process or system condition. Ask why the system allowed the error.
  • Root-cause methods: 5 Whys, cause-and-effect (fishbone) diagram, Pareto analysis.
  • Meridian Group continually improves using data, audits, and management review.

Do

  • Offer a cause, not only a description, when you report a problem.
  • Check later that an action worked, and record the result.
  • Share improvement ideas.

Do not

  • Stop at "operator error".
  • Use retraining as the only action.
  • Close an action without checking its effectiveness.

Clause reference

10.1, 10.2, 10.3

Quick check

Question. Define correction and corrective action.

Answer. Correction fixes the nonconforming output. Corrective action removes the cause so that it does not recur.

Module 10 Summary: Audits: What to Expect

This material is an original training aid. It is not a substitute for the standard. Obtain ISO 9001 from ISO or a national standards body.

Meridian Group one-page summary. Applies to: all roles (required).

Key points

  • Audit types: internal, customer, certification, surveillance, and recertification.
  • An audit runs through an opening meeting, evidence gathering, findings, a closing meeting, corrective action, and follow-up.
  • Auditors sample. Evidence carries more weight than statements.
  • Finding terms vary by certification body: major nonconformity, minor nonconformity, observation or opportunity for improvement. VERIFY

Do

  • Answer the question asked, truthfully.
  • Show the record or the instruction.
  • Say "I will find out" when unsure, and tell your supervisor.
  • Refer questions about another area to your supervisor.

Do not

  • Guess.
  • Volunteer unrelated information.
  • Argue about a finding on the spot.
  • Hide, tidy, or alter records.

Clause reference

9.2 (internal audit); certification audit practice is set by the certification body.

Quick check

Question. Why is a record stronger evidence than a statement?

Answer. A record was made at the time of the work and can be checked by someone else. A statement depends on memory.

Module 12 Summary: Capstone

This material is an original training aid. It is not a substitute for the standard. Obtain ISO 9001 from ISO or a national standards body.

Meridian Group one-page summary. Applies to: all roles (required).

Key points

  • The capstone checks that you can apply the program to real work.
  • Part 1 (30 minutes): 20-question quiz. Pass at 80 percent.
  • Part 2 (30 minutes): three written scenario responses: the issue, the immediate action, the clause, the preventive action.
  • Part 3 (10 minutes): observed demonstration of STOP, FLAG, HOLD, REPORT with a mock work item, and a document currency check.
  • A participant who does not pass receives coaching and retakes the parts not yet passed. Results go on the training record.

Do

  • Contain the problem first in every scenario.
  • Aim the preventive action at the cause, not at a person.
  • Perform the demonstration steps in order without prompting.

Do not

  • Start with re-measuring, rework, or investigation before containment.
  • Name "retrain the operator" as the only preventive action.

Clause reference

4 to 10 (all clauses).

Quick check

Question. In a scenario response, what comes first: the investigation or the immediate action?

Answer. The immediate action that contains the problem. Investigation follows.
